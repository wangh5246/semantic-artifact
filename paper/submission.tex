\title{SHTPC: Stateful History-to-Payload Closure for Private Heatmap Aggregation}
\author{}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Private heatmap aggregation over a predeclared client cohort requires authorizing hidden-state transitions without exposing client coordinates to the admission authority. However, composing zero-knowledge (ZK) state proofs with secret-shared aggregation leaves two semantic gaps, as a valid share vector can encode an unauthorized cell and aggregators can release an incomplete or distinguishing subset. This paper introduces Stateful History-to-Payload Closure (SHTPC) to bind hidden-state authorization to retained aggregator shares and complete-cohort first exposure. Exploiting the linearity of one-hot index moments over secret shares, each aggregator signs a masked-moment receipt over its retained share so admission verifies cell equality without in-circuit pseudorandom generator (PRG) seed expansion. Atomic admission and durable reservation further lock the complete eligible cohort before any honest aggregate share becomes decryptable, preserving accepted-history integrity across windows and crash recovery. Against an active aggregator colluding with the Collector, the joint protocol view is simulatable from public metadata and the declared complete-cohort histogram alone. At 10,001 bins, SHTPC requires 5,779 binding constraints versus 79,002,390 for in-circuit seed expansion, accelerating transition proving by 89.8--89.9$\times$ over Poseidon vector binding on server hardware and completing in 11.2\,s within 0.28\,GiB on a Raspberry Pi 4B with under 0.8\% binary wire overhead. In 100-client Janus runs, SHTPC incurs 1.44$\times$ batch latency (1.38$\times$ per session) over plaintext trusted generation while rejecting all tested cell mismatches and subset exposures.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A managed fleet with predeclared participants and coordinated sampling uses periodic cohort heatmaps for shift-end compliance auditing, depot quota settlement, and municipal SLA coverage verification without revealing individual driver trajectories~\cite{Popa2011PrivStats,Popa2009VPriv,Balasch2010PrETP}. Even when aggregation arithmetic is exact, semantic and membership mismatches distort operational decisions. Redirecting reports away from their history-authorized cells fabricates hotspots, inflating a T-Drive modal cell count from 3 to 22--23 when 20 of 100 reports are substituted in offline replay (Section~\ref{sec:eval-real-traces}), whereas omitting accepted reports suppresses true demand (Figure~\ref{fig:gap}). In regulatory auditing, omitting stragglers enables selective non-reporting of non-compliant routes, while post-preparation subset release creates differencing channels leaking individual locations (Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure-separation}).

Private aggregation validates individual inputs and combines them without
revealing any single input. The target application combines an admission
authority, which authorizes transitions without reading client coordinates,
with two aggregation roles that execute a native secret-shared protocol such as the
Distributed Aggregation Protocol (DAP)~\cite{Geoghegan2026DAP} with
Verifiable Distributed Aggregation Functions (VDAFs)~\cite{Barnes2023VDAF08,Corrigan2017Prio}. A Collector consumes the resulting
cohort statistics.
The remaining task is to connect the authorized transitions, retained
contributions and release membership to the same accepted history.

Existing protocols validate private inputs~\cite{Bell2023ACORN,Chowdhury2022EIFFeL,Rathee2023ELSA,Lycklama2023RoFL},
enforce inclusion or verify aggregates~\cite{Su2025SCIF,Acharya2025PEAR,Hahn2023VerSA},
and maintain private aggregate state~\cite{Ball2024Stateful}.
Each existing mechanism checks its own object in isolation.
When an application state proof authorizes cell \(p\) while a secret-shared aggregation report
encodes a conflicting cell \(q\ne p\), independent verification
accepts the cross-spliced pair. Superficial identifiers, signed bodies, or hiding commitments
associate inputs without binding the retained share vector. Because independent checks accept
the Cartesian product of locally valid objects, this vulnerability is structural (formalized in Appendix~\ref{sec:composition-failures}).

State freshness and release membership create two further interfaces. A proof
for predecessor \(c\) remains valid after a competing successor commits, so
proof verification does not authorize the current transition. Separately, an
accepted report dropped across both receiver channels yields a valid but incomplete
batch (Figure~\ref{fig:gap}).

Completeness also has a privacy deadline. Two legal histories can swap two
owners' positive cells while retaining the same public metadata and complete
heatmap. A subset containing only one of those owners has different aggregates.
Once the first honest share becomes decryptable, a corrupted aggregation role and
the Collector distinguish the histories, an exposure that later publication refusal
cannot retract (Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure-separation}). Accepted-history closure connects each admitted
claim to its retained contribution and legal state transition, and reconciles
the eligible set before first exposure.

Stateful History-to-Payload Closure (SHTPC) binds each hidden-state proof to
the share vector retained by the Aggregators without
reproducing native seed expansion inside the application circuit. Native
histogram validity restricts the reconstructed contribution to a one-hot
vector with a sentinel. Within this domain, its linear index moment identifies
the selected cell. Each role computes a masked receipt from its retained
output, and admission compares the paired receipts with the proof-bound masked
index. The application circuit is therefore independent of histogram width \(D\), while native
vector processing remains \(O(D)\).

The bridge introduces receipts and retry feedback that require joint analysis.
Authenticated source registration binds private processing to its registered
body, and joint simulation accounts for the resulting transcript. Atomic
admission advances the current history, while durable reservation fixes the
complete cohort before exposure. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} illustrates the four semantic boundaries connecting client claims to verified release. The threat model separates integrity from privacy, where integrity defends against malicious clients and untrusted transport, whereas privacy tolerates an actively corrupted aggregator colluding with the Collector. Both single-release and predeclared multi-window analyses establish simulation security given equal declared histograms for each permitted complete cohort.

The contributions can be summarized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Masked-moment binding.} Native one-hot validity and masked
receipts from retained outputs bind a hidden-state proof to the actual
contribution without reproducing native seed expansion in the application
circuit. The standalone relation has 5,779 constraints at 10,001 bins, versus
79,002,390 for direct seed binding, enabling 11.2\,s transition proving within
0.28\,GiB on a Raspberry Pi 4B with under 0.8\% binary wire overhead.

\item \emph{Accepted-history closure.} A security contract connects each
admitted claim, retained contribution and legal state transition to complete
eligible cohort membership at first exposure. A separation example shows why
publication refusal alone is too late. The construction maintains these
connections across retries and recovery under honest service execution.

\item \emph{Joint privacy of receipt feedback.} Masked receipts, rejection
feedback and retries are jointly simulatable with the native transcript
against an active aggregator colluding with the Collector. The simulator
receives no aggregate before the first honest share becomes decryptable and
one permitted complete-cohort output at that boundary. Simulation distinguishes malicious
sources' declarations from their actual retained outputs. Native controls
exercise these interfaces, and component and lifecycle measurements report
their costs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Model and Security Contract}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Deployment and Trust Model}
\label{sec:threat}

Enrolled clients report under stable pseudonymous identities. An admission
authority verifies hidden state proofs and maintains accepted history without
receiving coordinate openings or role decryption keys. Two Aggregators act
as the DAP Leader and Helper~\cite{Geoghegan2026DAP}, denoted \(S\in\{A,R\}\)
in formulas and as corrupted \(c\) and honest \(h\) in the privacy analysis. They
prepare input shares and retain aggregation outputs. The Collector holds the aggregate-share
Hybrid Public Key Encryption (HPKE) key, and its Decoder verifies release records and reconstructs the heatmap. The
authority-side Finalizer authenticates the frozen reference and its completion.
Its certificate joins that reference to both roles' matching attestations. A durable admission proxy
belongs to the same trust domain and delivers signed decisions. Each role
durably reserves its own share before export.

Integrity and privacy operate under distinct corruption models. For integrity, clients and transport are malicious, while admission, Aggregators, Finalizer, Collector and Decoder follow the protocol using serialized durable storage. This fits a managed fleet enforcing admission and release rules without granting the authority plaintext coordinates.
For privacy, one Aggregator may be actively corrupted in collusion with the Collector, additionally observing honest authority records, receipts, and diagnostics (excluding signing keys). Active Aggregators can forge shares or abort, targeting privacy rather than output integrity. Forged receipts provide no probing oracle, as admission acceptance for an honest report depends solely on the corrupt role's fixed original share and mask (Section~\ref{sec:joint-privacy}).

Privacy challenges evaluate pairs of legal histories sharing common public metadata \(\mu\), including client identities, task parameters, window indices, step and anchor references, eligibility, contribution bits and delivery schedules. The authority maintains no aggregate query interface, preventing differential reconstruction across intermediate updates. Definition~\ref{game:privacy} formalizes the single-release privacy contract with one visible cohort per task. Theorem~\ref{thm:multi-window-privacy} in Appendix~\ref{app:native-theory} extends this privacy guarantee to predeclared multi-window executions sharing state across windows. Reports and retained outputs each belong to one window, while later windows reference earlier accepted states as predecessors or anchors. Privacy bounds protocol leakage beyond permitted histograms and \(\mu\). Activity, contribution bits and cadence remain public, and exact outputs can support inference from auxiliary knowledge.

All guarantees assume correctly sampled public parameters with unknown trapdoors, reviewed fixed-width arithmetic, authenticated confidential registration and durable execution boundaries. Enrollment authenticates pseudonymous identities rather than physical Global Positioning System (GPS) locations or Sybil resistance, continuity is enforced over authenticated slots rather than unverified elapsed time, and authority equivocation lies outside this scope.

\subsection{Reports, Histories and Cohorts}
\label{sec:prelim-model}

Reports move through three distinct units of progress. A sampling slot assigns a measurement to time, an accepted step counts a committed transition, and a release window groups eligible reports. Rejected attempts do not advance accepted history.

Each report is projected and quantized under fixed public parameters, yielding a hidden vector \(\mathbf{x}_r\). A \(W\times H\) grid with cell width \(s\) selects
\begin{equation}
  p_r=\mathsf{Grid}(\mathbf{x}_r)
  =W\Bigl\lfloor\frac{y_r}{s}\Bigr\rfloor+\Bigl\lfloor\frac{x_r}{s}\Bigr\rfloor,
  \label{eq:canonical-grid}
\end{equation}
where \(D=WH\). The evaluated profile uses \(s=100\) and \(W=H=100\). The replay projection, rounding and boundary rules appear in Appendix~\ref{app:policy}.

All record maps are keyed by report identifier across the three lifecycle stages in Table~\ref{tab:record-types}.
First, in \emph{registration and locking}, clients register candidate bodies before preparation feedback, establishing registered history log \(P_w\) and its eligible projection \(R_w\). Warmup reports enter \(P_w\) to advance state counters but are excluded from \(R_w\), whereas eligible zero-bit records (\(b_r=0\)) remain in \(R_w\) despite adding no mass. Domain \(I_w=\operatorname{dom}(R_w)\) partitions into honest reports \(I_w^{\rm hon}\) and adversary reports \(I_w^{\rm adv}\). Projection \(\pi_{\rm reg}\) strips downstream receipts and commit signatures to compare core registered bodies.
Second, in \emph{admission commitment}, reports satisfying ZK state proofs, masked receipts, and atomic history checks yield committed log \(L_w\) and eligible map \(\mathcal A_w\).
Third, in \emph{role application}, roles \(S\in\{A,R\}\) process decisions into applied maps \(L_{S,w}\) and \(M_{S,w}\), retaining share vector \(v_{S,r}\in\mathbb F_q^{D+1}\) with sentinel bin. Decoding reserved sums \(\mathbf a_{S,w}\) reconstructs heatmap \(\hat{\mathbf h}_w\).

Strict release produces the complete eligible cohort or refuses, serving applications
that require coordinated sampling and can tolerate delayed batch release. Execution separates
failures across three distinct phases.
(1)~\emph{Pre-lock registration failure} occurs when a client fails to submit a complete signed
body or assign a required contiguous slot before the registration lock boundary.
(2)~\emph{Post-registration validation stall} occurs when an installed body contains an invalid
proof, references an uncommitted anchor, or violates quota.
(3)~\emph{Post-commit transport failure} occurs when network disconnection happens after an admission
decision is durably committed.
Once a complete request is registered and committed, subsequent client
disconnection does not halt cohort processing. Server-side durable redelivery and idempotent retry
continue role application and export autonomously without continuous client presence.
A member permanently missing during Phase~(1) registration or Phase~(2) validation withholds release,
prohibiting dynamic member dropouts or publishing over the receiver intersection set.

\subsection{Accepted-History Closure}
\label{sec:boundaries}
\label{sec:prelim-goals}
\label{sec:composition-summary}

Four obligations share \(A_r\). Payload binding (P1) connects the proved claim to the consumed
package by
\begin{equation}
\mathsf{Contrib}(\mathsf{pay}_{r,\mathrm{consumed}})
  =b_r\mathbf e_{p_r}.
  \label{eq:p1}
\end{equation}
Channel consistency (P2) requires both roles to consume channels of that same object. Atomic transition (P3) gives each
accepted object one atomic transition with its quota charge,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{State}_{r-1}\rightarrow\mathrm{State}_{r}.
  \label{eq:p3}
\end{equation}
Complete release (P4) requires a successful release to contain exactly the eligible committed
records and the complete locked reference,
\begin{equation}
  \pi_{\rm reg}(\mathcal A_w)=R_w.
  \label{eq:p4}
\end{equation}
These obligations organize the successful-trace and exposure-prefix
definitions below. Theorem~\ref{thm:main-semantic-closure} establishes their
composition under the stated integrity premises.

\begin{definition}[Legal sequential history]
\label{def:legal-history}
A sequence \(H=(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\) with unique identifiers is legal if replay
from enrolled genesis enables every transition. Each step has an authorized
owner and context, names the current head and policy-selected accepted anchor,
and satisfies the application relation on opened states. It atomically appends
the successor, advances the head and accepted-step counter, and charges quota
without changing other owners. Rejected and uncommitted attempts add no step.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[History refinement]
\label{def:history-refinement}
For a finite prefix, \(\mathcal L\preceq H\) when committed admission
operations bijectively map to a legal \(H\), preserving every \(A_r\) field
and the order of linearization points between invocation and response.
Committed pending operations are completed at the prefix end, while
uncommitted attempts are discarded. Retries of one durable decision represent
one operation, and distinct committed effects remain distinct.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Successful-trace closure]
\label{def:trace-closure}
Fix a transition policy, additive contribution map \(r\mapsto m_r\) and
eligibility rule. A successful release has closure if some legal \(H\)
satisfies \(\mathcal L\preceq H\). Let \(E_w(H)\) be the set of records in
the complete accepted history \(H\) that belong to window \(w\) and satisfy
the eligibility rule. Their identifiers equal \(\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal A_w)\),
and each identifies one common admitted object across proof, packages,
transition and certificate. Each role's application count \(N_{S,w}(r)\) satisfies
\(N_{S,w}(r)=\mathbf1[r\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal A_w)]\), and its reserved sum uses exactly
those retained outputs. The pre-noise output is
\(\sum_{r\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal A_w)}m_r\). For heatmaps,
\(m_r=b_r\mathbf e_{\mathsf{Grid}(\mathbf x_r)}\). Eligibility is independent
of bit value.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Exposure-prefix safety]
\label{def:exposure-prefix}
Every decryptable honest share comes from a prior durable reservation
satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \pi_{\rm reg}(L_{S,w})=P_w,\qquad
 \pi_{\rm reg}(M_{S,w})=R_w=\pi_{\rm reg}(\mathcal A_w),\\
 \mathbf a_{S,w}=\sum_{r\in I_w}v_{S,r}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:export-safety}
\end{equation}
Retries preserve its membership and plaintext. Successful-trace closure also
requires correct final reconstruction.
\end{definition}

\section{SHTPC Design}
\label{sec:system}

SHTPC realizes P1--P4 by connecting hidden-state authorization to
retained aggregation outputs and complete first exposure.
\subsection{Design Overview}
\label{sec:architecture-choice}
\label{sec:overview}

Anchor Distance Window Continuity (ADWC) bounds each hidden move relative to the last accepted position and historical anchor. The client proves ADWC and grid membership in zero knowledge, while native validity and masked-moment receipts bind the proved cell to retained aggregator outputs. Admission verifies receipts against accepted history.

\subsection{End-to-End Workflow}
Enrollment fixes owner keys, a proved hidden genesis and task configuration. Each window proceeds through five steps.
(1)~\emph{Lock.} Clients register complete signed bodies. The authority verifies the candidate graph and locks \(P_w, R_w\) before private preparation or receipt feedback.
(2)~\emph{Retain.} Both roles install the lock. Authorized by permits referencing current heads, each role evaluates its input, retains its native output, and signs a masked-moment receipt.
(3)~\emph{Commit.} Admission verifies paired receipts against Eq.~\eqref{eq:receipt-simulation}. An atomic transaction rechecks heads, anchors and quota, persisting successor states and signed decisions.
(4)~\emph{Apply.} A durable proxy delivers decisions. Each role applies each decision once, updating retained history and eligible maps while warmups advance history without aggregation eligibility.
(5)~\emph{Reserve.} Each role verifies applied records against the locked reference \(\pi_{\rm reg}(M_{S,w}) = R_w\), durably reserving the complete sum and selector before transmission. Retries reuse the identical snapshot.

\subsection{Binding Hidden State to Native Outputs}
\label{sec:native-adaptor}

P1 binds the state-authorized cell to the retained native output without encoding
native pseudorandom generator (PRG) seed expansion inside the ZK circuit. Under native private
aggregation, the underlying verifiable aggregation layer enforces one-hot validity of
the reconstructed vector (\cite[Section~7.4.4]{Barnes2023VDAF08}). For roles $A$ and $R$
retaining shares $v_A, v_R \in \mathbb F_q^{D+1}$, the reconstructed output satisfies
$v = v_A + v_R = \mathbf e_y$ for a unique unit coordinate $y \in [0, D]$.

Over this one-hot domain, the linear index moment $\sum_{j=0}^D j v[j] = y$ uniquely
identifies the selected index $y$ (for example, in a three-cell vector $(0,0,1)$,
$0\cdot0+1\cdot0+2\cdot1=2$). In native field $\mathbb F_q$ with $D < q$, let a state-derived
primary cell $p < D$ and public bit $b$ authorize target index $x = bp + (1-b)D$, with sentinel $D$.
The client samples private uniform masks $r_A, r_R \in \mathbb F_q$, commits to their openings,
and encrypts $r_S$ for role $S \in \{A, R\}$. The ZK proof binds $x$ and the masks to
the public target
\begin{equation}
 T = x + r_A + r_R \pmod q.
 \label{eq:native-target}
\end{equation}
Each correct role computes its masked moment from the retained output as
\begin{equation}
 m_S = \sum_{j=0}^{D} j v_S[j] + r_S \pmod q.
 \label{eq:native-moment}
\end{equation}
By linearity, summing the two receipts gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 m_A + m_R &= \sum_{j=0}^D j (v_A[j] + v_R[j]) + r_A + r_R \\
           &= y + r_A + r_R \pmod q.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:moment-cancellation}
\end{equation}
Consequently, checking $m_A + m_R = T$ directly tests $y = x \pmod q$. Lemma~\ref{lem:native-moment-fidelity}
shows that acceptance forces the reconstructed contribution to equal $b\mathbf e_p + (1-b)\mathbf e_D$,
recovering $b\mathbf e_p$ upon sentinel removal. Table~\ref{tab:binding-designs} compares
this approach against generic vector binding and direct seed binding.

\noindent\textbf{Transition circuit relation (ADWC and grid).}
The public statement binds the state commitments, report context and masked
index. Its witness opens the state, ticket, mask and receipt commitments.
The relation binds the payload witness's cell and
remainder to the successor and enforces
\(p=\mathsf{Grid}(\mathbf x_r)\),
\(\|\mathbf x_r-\mathbf x_{\rm prev}\|^2\le\tau_u^2\Delta t_t^2\) and
\(\|\mathbf x_r-\mathbf a_t\|^2\le
\min\{\tau_u^2(\Delta T_t^{(K)})^2,c_{\rm policy}^2\}\).
Here \(\mathbf a_t\) is the policy-selected accepted anchor at lag \(K\),
\(\Delta t_t\) and \(\Delta T_t^{(K)}\) are the slot-time gaps to the predecessor
and the anchor, \(\tau_u\) is the
speed tier and \(c_{\rm policy}\) is the anchor cap. Admission checks that the
referenced predecessor and anchor are the actual accepted ones. The same grid
and distance constraints apply when \(b=0\). Appendix~\ref{app:policy} gives
the parameters, and the artifact's fixed transition circuit gives the ordered
public-signal interface (Section~\ref{sec:artifact}).

\noindent\textbf{Cross-field canonical encoding (\(\mathbb F_q\) vs.\ \(p_{\rm proof}\)).}
To bridge native field \(\mathbb F_q\) and proof scalar field \(p_{\rm proof}\),
canonical scalars enforce \(x+r_A+r_R=T+\chi q\) with carry \(\chi\in\{0,1,2\}\) over integers,
preventing wrap-around between disparate field moduli (Appendix~\ref{app:native-interface}).
State commitments retain birth domains and references, and admission verifies the
masked-moment sum without receiving coordinate openings or Aggregator decryption keys.

\noindent\textbf{Complexity and idempotent preparation.}
Because moment checking operates on scalars while share evaluation scans \(D+1\) buckets,
the application circuit size is independent of histogram width \(D\), whereas native vector
processing remains \(O(D)\). Separating share preparation from accumulation ensures that each
signed receipt authenticates the package context and retained share, with exact retries
replaying the same output and receipt.

The compact binding relation relies on the injectivity of index moments over one-hot vectors $\mathbf e_y$. While a first-order moment is non-injective on general vectors (for example, $(0, 2, 0)$ and $(1, 0, 1)$ both evaluate to 2), multi-dimensional or weighted stateful applications can apply higher-order or component-wise moments whenever their reconstructed domain admits an injective moment relation.

\subsection{Protecting the Feedback Interface}
\label{sec:origin-feedback}
\label{sec:cross-role}

Encryption does not authenticate an input's owner. An attacker can transplant
an honest ciphertext and substitute a known mask, causing an honest receipt to
reveal the cell before admission rejects the report. This feedback arises from
the new bridge even when the proof and native protocol are secure in isolation.

SHTPC allocates an owner identifier to one complete signed body and registers
it before private processing. Each ingress authenticates the consumed metadata,
ciphertexts, sidecars, installed lock and permit against that body. Both
retained sessions and receipts therefore refer to the same admitted object,
realizing P2. Source registration, origin locking, and fixed original shares prevent
an active adversary from splicing components across reports or adapting receipts to
probe honest state. Because the registered body fixes the original share and mask,
and retry and abort states are serialized, adaptive feedback
cannot alter the receipt acceptance condition.

Recovery retains the same source and native state. The Helper journals its
first protected input and response before sending. Identical retries replay
those bytes, while changed inputs or new jobs cannot reopen that branch. The
Leader's lease and transaction fence permit at most one committed transition.
Uncommitted computation can receive new peer feedback, which the joint privacy
analysis includes explicitly.

\subsection{Atomic History Authorization}
\label{sec:admission}

A transition proof can remain valid after another successor commits. Admission
therefore verifies the proof before the transaction, then atomically rechecks
the actual head, anchor and quota and stores the successor, history and signed
decision. Both retained-output checks
precede this linearization point. Preparation, admission and role application
use separate databases connected by that decision and its output links.

For stable identity \(u\), task and window \(w\), durable quota
\(q_{u,w}\in\{0,1\}\) is shared across credential epochs. With accepted step
\(t\) and \(N_{\rm warm}=\max(K,2)\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_t&=b_t\mathbf1[t>N_{\rm warm}],\\
q_{u,w}+\gamma_t&\le1,\qquad
q_{u,w}\gets q_{u,w}+\gamma_t.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:window-quota}
\end{equation}
Commit induction gives
\begin{equation}
q_{u,w}=\sum_{\substack{r\in\mathcal A_w\\\mathsf{uid}(r)=u}} b_r\le1.
\label{eq:quota-invariant}
\end{equation}
Warmups remain in the transition log without charge. Eligible zero-bit updates
remain members and can advance state after quota is spent.

History is keyed by stable \((\mathsf{task},\mathsf{uid})\), with credential, revision, window, receipt and budget checks inside the transaction. Credential epochs and storage revisions do not replace accepted-step counters or sampling slots. The proxy queues exact decisions durably before delivery. Role databases apply each decision once, including out-of-order delivery, while retries return that decision and credential rotation preserves history and spent quota.

\subsection{Complete Exposure and Recovery}
\label{sec:finalization}
\label{sec:strict-choice}
P4 fixes membership before preparation feedback can influence it. Admission
locks the verified candidate graph and its eligible projection at the public
registration boundary. The graph includes warmups and eligible zeros together
with their predecessor and anchor references. Preparation errors and receipts cannot select a smaller cohort or replace
a locked body. Export by role \(S\) requires
\begin{equation}
 \pi_{\rm reg}(L_{S,w})=P_w,\qquad \pi_{\rm reg}(M_{S,w})=R_w.
 \label{eq:final-report-set}
\end{equation}
These equalities compare the frozen registration cores, including context and
body. The role separately checks each actual retained-output link against its
authenticated receipt and commit decision.

In one export transaction, the role verifies Eq.~\eqref{eq:final-report-set} and reserves the
selector, record map, retained-output links, and exact cohort sum
(\label{sec:profiles}\label{sec:dp}under the strict profile) before sending its share.
Because role application is monotone (\(0 \to 1\)), this atomic check refines
Definition~\ref{def:trace-closure} by ensuring all locked members are applied before exposure (Appendix~\ref{boundary:appendix}).
Persisting the resulting snapshot before transmission ensures that re-exports idempotently
re-emit the identical complete sum without altering membership. Warmup completion is checked
through \(L_{S,w}\), and eligible zeros remain in \(M_{S,w}\). Responses and re-encrypted retries
read the same durable snapshot, so recovery cannot change its plaintext or
membership. The Finalizer authenticates the frozen reference and matching attestations, and
the Decoder checks that certificate and reserved records before reconstruction.

After locking, any member failing registration or validation holds or aborts the
cohort without silent exclusion or intersection release (Section~\ref{sec:prelim-model}).
Conversely, committed decisions survive client disconnection via autonomous
server-side redelivery without re-upload. Under a hypothetical sensitivity model assuming each locked member independently completes registration, validation, and role application before the deadline with probability \(p\), monolithic release occurs with probability \(P_{\rm release} = p^N\).
For illustrative values \(p = 0.999\) and \(p = 0.98\), monolithic \(P_{\rm release}\) reaches \(96.8\%\) (\(N=32\)) and \(90.5\%\) (\(N=100\)) at \(p = 0.999\), dropping to \(13.3\%\) (\(N=100\)) at \(p = 0.98\).
Partitioning \(N\) into \(M\) fixed regional or shift sub-cohorts of size \(k = N/M\) leaves simultaneous full-fleet completion at \((p^k)^M = p^N\), while raising each sub-cohort's independent release probability \(p^k\) at \(p = 0.98\) to \(85.1\%\) (\(k=8\)) or \(60.3\%\) (\(k=25\)), allowing completed sub-cohorts to release localized totals at a smaller anonymity set \(k\).
Pre-exposure abort retains partial commits, heads and quota for continuation as in Table~\ref{tab:lifecycle}.
Incompatible schedules are rejected at the lock boundary, ensuring task configuration remains durably bound to the database. Theorem~\ref{thm:multi-window-privacy} in Appendix~\ref{app:native-theory} establishes multi-window privacy.

\label{prop:native-post-admission-progress}
For a finite fully committed lock, eventual service and channel recovery with
fair retries and no deadline stop gives complete release. Each successful apply
removes one of at most \(2|P_w|\) pending role-record pairs. Permanent client refusal and incomplete pre-lock registration fall outside
these progress conditions. Admission caps bound accepted work and exact retries
consume no new slot. The native profile uses \(B_u=16\) per owner and window.

\begin{definition}[Closure game]
\label{game:closure}
The challenger initializes honest keys, enrolled identities and genesis state under serializable durable execution. A bounded PPT adversary controls malicious clients and delivery, submitting packages, observing responses, reordering or suppressing traffic, and invoking admission and closure, while honest signing keys and committed state remain uncorrupted. The challenger records locked \(P_w,R_w\), invocation events, committed objects and receiver counts. The adversary wins if the Decoder accepts a release lacking a legal linearization, if any membership, application-count, common-object or reconstruction condition of Definition~\ref{def:trace-closure} fails, or if a role exports an unsafe share violating Eq.~\eqref{eq:export-safety}. Refusal without an unsafe export is not a win. Write \(\operatorname{Adv}_{\rm closure}(\mathcal B)=\Pr[\mathsf{Win}]\) under the strict profile.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Native moment fidelity]
\label{lem:native-moment-fidelity}
Assume sound relation certification, commitment binding, authenticated common
body identity, correct receivers and native validity. Let the certified
canonical coordinate be \(\mathbf x_r\), with \(p=\mathsf{Grid}(\mathbf x_r)<D<q\)
and \(b\in\{0,1\}\). Let \(x=bp+(1-b)D\), with canonical
\(r_A,r_R,T\in[0,q-1]\), and \(F(v)=\sum_{j=0}^{D}jv[j]\). Suppose the
retained outputs satisfy \(v_A+v_R=\mathbf e_y\) and the receipts satisfy
\(m_S=F(v_S)+r_S\).
If certification fixes \(T=x+r_A+r_R\pmod q\) and admission checks
\(m_A+m_R=T\), the reconstructed contribution after sentinel removal is
\(b\mathbf e_p\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Opening checks fix the certified masks. Linearity gives
\(y=F(v_A+v_R)=T-r_A-r_R=x\pmod q\).
Since \(x,y\in[0,D]\) and \(D<q\), this is integer equality, and
removing sentinel \(D\) yields \(b\mathbf e_p\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[First-exposure separation]
\label{prop:exposure-separation}
Under the share-observation interface of Section~\ref{sec:threat}, suppose
two metadata-compatible admissible histories have the same aggregate
on a full accepted set \(\mathcal C\), but different aggregates on a permitted native
batch \(R\subsetneq\mathcal C\). Native validity, atomic admission and exact-set
checking at final publication do not imply exposure-prefix safety or privacy
relative to the full aggregate. This holds even for a wrapper that successfully
publishes \(\mathcal C\) on complete delivery.
\end{proposition}
The separating execution delivers complementary shares of \(R\) to the
corrupted role and Collector, then refuses publication because \(R\ne\mathcal C\).
As constructed in Appendix~\ref{app:exposure-separation}, this exposure distinguishes the legal histories despite subsequent publication refusal.

\par\noindent\emph{Related-setup extraction.}
Because privacy simulation generates honest-source proofs while an adversary can combine or re-randomize material across related setup keys, single-proof knowledge soundness does not specify the required source-reuse interface. The analysis works in typed generic bilinear groups without a target-to-source map under correct setups with unknown trapdoors, ceremonies with at least one honest contribution, nonzero independent phase-two scalars, and finite budgets (Appendix~\ref{app:primitive-reductions}). Only current genesis and transition namespaces are accepted, while auxiliary keys remain adversary-visible. Statements are registered before sampling fresh proof coins by key namespace, relation and ordered public signals, caching known or extracted witnesses without offering witness-free proof service to malicious owners. The extraction argument applies to circuit compilation layouts satisfying the structural unit-row condition. Specifically, each compiled target key appends dedicated \(A\)-only unit rows (\(B=C=0\)) for its \(\ell_r+1\) public wires (6 for genesis, 26 for transition). On the corresponding evaluation domain roots \(z_j\), each public polynomial satisfies \(u_{r,m}(z_j)=\mathbf 1[m=j]\) while private wire polynomials vanish (\(u_{r,e}(z_j)=0\)). This structural property holds for the compiled artifacts (Section~\ref{sec:artifact-proofs}) rather than unconstrained Groth16 circuits.

\begin{lemma}[Related-setup extraction]
\label{lem:related-crs-extraction}
Under these source rules and compiled unit-row conditions, every accepted proof yields a witness or reuses the
statement of a registered source with identical key, relation and ordered public signals,
except with probability at most \(g\).
Appendix~\ref{app:primitive-reductions} gives its finite budget.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For public polynomial \(Q_k(\phi)=\sum_{m=0}^{\ell_k}\phi_m(\beta_k u_{k,m}+\alpha_k v_{k,m}+w_{k,m})\) (\(\phi_0=1\)), each fresh source \(i\) under key \(r_i\) registers independent \((\mu_i,\nu_i)\) and \(([\mu_i]_1,[\nu_i]_2,[S_i]_1)\) with \(S_i=(\mu_i\nu_i-\alpha_{r_i}\beta_{r_i}-Q_{r_i}(\phi_i))/\Delta_{r_i}\). Any typed outputs \((A,B,C)\in\mathbb G_1\times\mathbb G_2\times\mathbb G_1\) expand over setup and imported handles \((A_0,B_0,C_0)\) plus all allowed source handles as \(A=A_0+\sum_i a_i\mu_i+\sum_i b_i S_i\), \(B=B_0+\sum_j z_j\nu_j\), and \(C=C_0+\sum_i \hat a_i\mu_i+\sum_i \hat b_i S_i\). Outside generic collision probability \(g\), target verification under key \(r\) enforces \(AB=\alpha\beta+Q_r(\phi)+\Delta_r C\). Degree-three \(\mu_i\nu_i\nu_j\) terms give \(b_i z_j=0\), and if some \(b_i\neq 0\), \(\mu_i\nu_i\) forces \(B_0=\lambda\Delta_r\) and \(\hat b_k=\lambda b_k\), leaving \(\Delta_r(\lambda A_0-C_0)=\alpha\beta+Q_r(\phi)\) with no \(\Delta_r\)-free \(\alpha\beta\) term since CRS \(\Delta_r^{-1}\) numerators are linear in \((\alpha,\beta)\), forcing \(b_i=0\). Comparing \(\mu_i\nu_j\), \(\nu_j\), and \(\mu_i\) yields \(a_i z_j=0\) (\(i\neq j\)), \(a_i z_i=\hat b_i\Delta_r/\Delta_{r_i}\), \(z_j A_0=0\), and \(a_i B_0=\hat a_i\Delta_r\). If some \(a_{i^*}z_{i^*}\neq 0\), independent \(\Delta_{r_i}\) force \(r_{i^*}=r\), \(A_0=0\), \(B_0=\tau\Delta_r\) with \(\hat a_{i^*}=\tau a_{i^*}\), and \(a_{i^*}z_{i^*}=\hat b_{i^*}=1\), covering re-randomizations such as \((a_{i^*}A_{i^*},\,a_{i^*}^{-1}B_{i^*}+\tau[\Delta_r]_2,\,C_{i^*}+a_{i^*}\tau A_{i^*})\) without requiring identical proof bytes, while evaluating at the \(A\)-only public unit roots in \(Q_r(\phi)-Q_r(\phi_{i^*})+\Delta_r C_0=0\) isolates each \(\phi_m-\phi_{i^*,m}=0\) since private \(u\)-polynomials vanish, forcing \(\phi=\phi_{i^*}\). Otherwise one-sided \(a_i\) or \(z_j\) terms vanish by the same \(\alpha\beta\) check, forcing \(a_i=z_j=\hat a_i=\hat b_i=0\), and \(\Delta_k\)-separation plus divisibility by \(Z_r(X)\) extracts an explicit witness (Appendix~\ref{app:primitive-reductions}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Accepted-history composition at first exposure]
\label{thm:main-semantic-closure}

adopts the one-wayness assumption for Secure Hash Algorithm 256 (SHA256) on uniform 128-bit keys with resource-bounded
advantage \(a_{\rm OW128}\). Then
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Adv}_{\rm closure}\le\min\{1,B_C+v_N\}.
 \label{eq:primitive-bounds}
\end{equation}
Concrete parameterizations, query budgets, and algebraic expansions for \(B_C\)

\subsection{Joint Privacy with Feedback}
\label{sec:joint-privacy}

\begin{definition}[Single-cohort privacy experiment]
\label{game:privacy}
Fix a finite public resource budget before setup. Sample a correct setup and
fix the task, profile, keys and static corruption set before any honest
measurement. The budget covers honest and
adversarial work, including warmups, zero-bit reports, failed and unprepared
reports, verification, oracle calls, bytes and retries. Execution stops at the
budget. The environment supplies two complete legal histories \(H_0,H_1\) for
honest sources with the same public skeleton \(\mu\). It fixes each source's
challenge inputs before its first report, while the adversary can adaptively
request reports from those histories. Honest reports reach admission over an
independent authenticated confidential channel according to the input-ready
plan. Local witness and proof generation time is outside the online view.

For \(X\in\{A,P,P{+}A\}\), the challenger selects a uniform hidden bit \(d\), runs
the protocol on \(H_d\), and interacts with a bounded PPT adversary
\(\mathcal B\) with the following view. In \(A\),
the correct authority's view includes its business records, receipts,
diagnostics, database and backups, and its ordinary authorization secrets.
It has no role decryption keys or aggregate output. The observer may compute
offline with its keys but cannot use them to issue extra authority signatures,
change the configuration or bypass the state machine. In \(P\), one actively corrupted
Aggregator and the Collector share their state, decrypted inputs, peer
messages and actual available Collector results. The corrupted role can
fabricate messages and shares or abort. In \(P{+}A\), that observer also sees
the correct authority's business view, without its authorization secret or
malicious issuance. The adversary can control corrupted owners and schedule,
delay, replay or drop messages within the model. Each view includes its
visible proofs, ciphertexts, commitments, responses, refusals, retries, aborts
and modeled online times. These outcomes are not assumed to be part of \(\mu\).

For each record, let \(y_i^{(d)}=b_i p_i^{(d)}+(1-b_i)D\) denote its declared native index for primary cell \(p_i^{(d)}\). For each allowed complete cohort \(R_w\), the ideal interface returns declared complete-cohort histogram \(\mathcal O_d(R_w)=\sum_{i\in I_w}\mathbf e_{y_i^{(d)}}\) with sentinel \(D\) (written \(\mathcal O(R_w)\) when clear). Because corrupted parties can alter shares, \(\mathcal O_d(R_w)\) sums declared indices rather than reconstructed outputs. The \(A\) simulator receives no aggregate query and requires no histogram equality. The \(P\) and \(P{+}A\) histories have equal declared complete-cohort histograms for every allowed complete cohort, and their simulators may query that full cohort once when the first honest share becomes decryptable, returning no output earlier. Retries reuse the reserved plaintext without additional queries, and subset queries are forbidden.

The adversary outputs a bit in \(\mathsf{Exp}_X^d\). Its distinguishing
advantage is \(\operatorname{Adv}_X(\mathcal B)=
|\Pr[\mathsf{Exp}_X^1(\mathcal B)=1]-
\Pr[\mathsf{Exp}_X^0(\mathcal B)=1]|\).
\end{definition}

Theorem~\ref{thm:multi-window-privacy} (Appendix~\ref{app:native-theory}) extends this model via private prefetch, allowing the simulator to query \(\mathcal O(R_w)\) upon durable reservation before transmission while caching the result across aborts and recovery so all observable prefixes match exposure-time evaluation.

\begin{theorem}[Joint privacy of admission and aggregation views]
\label{thm:joint-privacy-main}
Fix the privacy experiment of Definition~\ref{game:privacy} with static corruption,
the strict profile, and the source and programmable-oracle models in
Appendix~\ref{app:primitive-reductions}. The authority view is simulatable without honest coordinates or an aggregate.
The aggregation view, including any shared authority business view, is simulatable
from the observer's inputs, \(\mu\) and the permitted complete-cohort histogram. The distinguishing advantage of each observer \(X\) is at most
\(\operatorname{Adv}_X\le\min\{1,2e_X\}\), where \(e_X\) composes the
primitive bounds above, the hidden HPKE encapsulations and the native
stream simulation. Theorem~\ref{thm:native-single-batch-privacy} in Appendix~\ref{app:native-theory} gives the
concrete form and a hybrid outline.
\end{theorem}

The masked receipts provide the key link. Let \(c\) be the actively corrupted role
and \(h\) the honest role. For an honest source report, the honest role computes
\(m_h = F(v_h^{\rm orig}) + r_h\) over its retained share, where \(F(v) = \sum_j j v[j]\).
Admission accepts paired receipts \((m_h, z)\) if and only if \(m_h + z = T\). Because
\(T = x + r_h + r_c \pmod q\) and native one-hot validity gives \(F(v_h^{\rm orig}) + F(v_c^{\rm orig}) = x\),
an arbitrary corrupt receipt \(z\) passes admission if and only if
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 z &= T - m_h = (x + r_h + r_c) - (F(v_h^{\rm orig}) + r_h) \\
   &= F(v_c^{\rm orig}) + r_c \pmod q.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:receipt-simulation}
\end{equation}
Because \(x\), \(v_h^{\rm orig}\), and \(r_h\) cancel in Eq.~\eqref{eq:receipt-simulation} and authenticated registration locks \(v_c^{\rm orig}, r_c\) to the immutable body before role processing, acceptance under \((v_c^{\rm orig}, r_c, T, m_h, \mathsf{status})\) depends solely on the corrupt role's own share and mask rather than honest trajectories. Sampling uniform \(T\) therefore fixes \(m_h = T - F(v_c^{\rm orig}) - r_c\) before learning the cell, while the authority-only simulator samples uniform \((T, U)\) to set \((m_A, m_R) = (U, T-U)\) without an aggregate query.

The joint simulator maintains four persistent state tables including \(\mathsf{Reg}\) for registered simulated source statements and handles, \(\mathsf{Dec}\) for non-challenge CCA sidecar decryptions, \(\mathsf{Share}\) for honest-role first-input journals, and \(\mathsf{Lock}\) for frozen \((P_w, R_w)\) cohort references. Active retries, transmission failures, and malformed submissions route through explicit branches preserving joint distribution invariance. Under corrupted Leader \(A\), honest Helper \(R\) records its first valid preparation output \((r_R, \mathrm{prep}_R)\) in \(\mathsf{Share}\) upon receiving report \((w, \mathsf{rid})\), returning cached receipts on replay and refusing mutated shares under the invariant in Eq.~\eqref{eq:receipt-simulation}. Pre-exposure reservation withholds honest aggregate share \(\mathbf a_{R,w}\) until \(\pi_{\rm reg}(M_{R,w}) = R_w\), preventing incomplete-subset exposure. Under corrupted Helper \(R\), Leader \(A\) executes uncommitted retries under atomic database lease fencing, evaluating identical fixed inputs \(v_c^{\rm orig}, r_c\) against invariant \(\Sigma = z_h + z_c^{\rm orig}\) so that aborted transactions leave no committed head or quota delta. This routing permits malicious sources without providing an oracle for an honest source's challenge.

For an honest source, the coupled receipt in Eq.~\eqref{eq:receipt-simulation}
uses the same native share as the preparation transcript. For a malicious
source, let \(y_j\) be its extracted or known claimed index and \(w_{h,j}\)
the actual successful output of the honest role. The simulator does not assume
that these outputs satisfy the honest-source identity. When reservation permits
first exposure, the single complete-cohort query \(\mathcal O(R_w)\) gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 V_{h,R_w}={}&\mathcal O(R_w)-\sum_{j\in I_w^{\rm adv}}\mathbf e_{y_j}\\
 &-\sum_{i\in I_w^{\rm hon}}v_{c,i}^{\rm orig}
 +\sum_{j\in I_w^{\rm adv}}w_{h,j}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:simulated-honest-export}
\end{equation}
Subtracting malicious declarations \(\mathbf e_{y_j}\) isolates the honest-source sum, subtracting \(v_{c,i}^{\rm orig}\) converts it to honest-role shares \(v_{h,i}=\mathbf e_{y_i}-v_{c,i}^{\rm orig}\), and adding \(w_{h,j}\) incorporates malicious-source outputs without subset queries or honest-share assumptions, with retries reusing the reserved plaintext.

Appendix~\ref{app:native-theory} gives the Histogram kernel and reduction
budgets for the joint simulation.

\begin{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work-summary}

Positioning SHTPC among related architectures requires distinguishing standard transaction primitives (atomic compare-and-swap heads, idempotent outbox queues, durable reservations) from cryptographic interface properties. SHTPC uses standard primitives for state management. The research contribution lies in resolving the cryptographic bottlenecks that emerge when an external state ledger and pre-exposure release gates are attached to verifiable aggregation.

\subsection{Input validity, inclusion and verifiable aggregation}
VDAFs and DAP~\cite{Addanki2022PrioPlus,Barnes2023VDAF08,Geoghegan2026DAP,Davis2023VDAFPrivacy} decouple validation from aggregation, extending to heavy hitters and streams~\cite{Boneh2021Poplar,Mouris2025Mastic,Rathee2024Whisper}. ELSA, RoFL and fully linear PCPs enforce input validity on shared data~\cite{Rathee2023ELSA,Lycklama2023RoFL,Boneh2019FullyLinear}, while VerSA and VeriFL verify aggregate correctness~\cite{Hahn2023VerSA,Luo2024VerSAComments,Guo2021VeriFL,Guo2022VeriFLFix}. Commit-and-prove SNARKs~\cite{Campanelli2019LegoSNARK} and collaborative SNARKs~\cite{Ozdemir2022Collaborative} link proofs to data, but length-\(D\) commitments impose \(O(D)\) curve operations per report.
Retrofitting an external ledger onto existing architectures highlights these costs. Retaining Prio3 aggregators requires binding client Groth16 proofs to secret shares, where in-circuit seed expansion (Method~S) requires 79.0M constraints, and hashing all \(D\) elements via Poseidon (Method~V) expands the complete transition circuit to 851,715 constraints requiring 13.9--14.1\,GiB prover memory on server hardware (exceeding 1.8\,GiB edge-device memory capacity). In contrast, the full SHTPC transition circuit contains 13,327 constraints, and proof generation uses 0.30\,GiB RSS on the server platform, with standalone masked-moment binding using 5,779 constraints and edge proving completing within 0.28\,GiB.
PEAR~\cite{Acharya2025PEAR} provides public-key aggregation with degree-2 NP validity under a single untrusted server using class-group inner product functional encryption (IPFE). Under the same external ledger, encoding private history transitions inside PEAR's IPFE witness expands encrypted vectors beyond \(L=3D+3=30{,}006\) slots into additional class-group operations. Conversely, SHTPC's external SNARK moment check does not transfer directly to PEAR's single-server model, because unmasked linear moments reveal plaintext coordinates to the single server whereas SHTPC's additive-mask receipt relies on split-trust roles.
SCIF~\cite{Su2025SCIF} achieves Byzantine fault tolerance (\(<1/3\) malicious servers) on memoryless inputs, validating static predicates before aggregation and Output Party reconstruction without external per-identity history state or pre-exposure complete-cohort locking. Candidate extensions to enforce hidden-state histories include compiling state-chain checks into four-party distributed Ligero or adapting masked-moment receipts across SCIF's servers with joint complaint simulation, paired with pre-exposure cohort reservation.

\subsection{Stateful workflows, credentials and membership}
Proof-carrying data~\cite{Chiesa2010PCD,Bitansky2012Recursive} propagates computation history, while Privacy Pass, DAP report authentication, zk-creds and ZEBRA authorize credentials~\cite{Davidson2024PrivacyPass,Priebe2023ReportAuth,Rosenberg2023ZKCreds,Rathee2022Zebra}. Ball et al.~\cite{Ball2024Stateful} track global state rather than private histories, leaving secret-share binding and subset exposure unaddressed. In location services, private schemes enforce charges, quotas or regional membership without revealing trajectories~\cite{Popa2009VPriv,Balasch2010PrETP,Popa2011PrivStats,Ernstberger2025ZKLP}. Static credentials do not prevent replay against stale state, whereas SHTPC atomically authorizes successors against the actual history head. Unlike federated schemes that tolerate dropouts via dynamic subsets~\cite{Bonawitz2017SecureAggregation,Ma2023Flamingo,Bell2020SecAggPlus,So2022LightSecAgg}, SHTPC enforces strict release, freezing complete eligible cohorts before first share exposure and handling transport faults through durable redelivery.

\subsection{Output privacy}
Cryptographic aggregation shields individual payloads and intermediate views from untrusted aggregators, yet cohort totals can disclose information across correlated queries. Differential privacy~\cite{Dwork2014algorithmic,Bagdasaryan2022SparseHeatmaps} provides orthogonal protection by injecting calibrated perturbation into aggregate releases. Central differential privacy adds noise upon decoding, whereas distributed perturbation generates secret-shared randomness across roles prior to export. Because the quota invariant in Eq.~\eqref{eq:quota-invariant} limits each identity to at most one unit contribution per window, \(L_1\) sensitivity remains bounded, simplifying noise calibration, preventing dropouts from altering the accounted cohort, and complementing protocol-view simulation across windows.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper incorporates hidden-state validation into native private aggregation, binding each admitted transition to the contribution retained by aggregation roles via native one-hot validity and compact masked moments, extended to complete-cohort first exposure through atomic admission and durable reservation. End-to-end Janus controls exercise these interfaces, while benchmarks and lifecycle measurements quantify their costs.

The design targets coordinated sampling requiring complete cohort statistics with delayed batch release, preserving strict membership without silent dropouts while durable server redelivery handles disconnections. All guarantees apply to admitted claims under the stated models without establishing physical GPS truth or Sybil resistance, and differencing attacks on exact cohort totals require orthogonal output-level differential privacy. Future directions include continuous sliding-window streaming with adaptive network recovery, generalizing masked moments to high dimensions, and recursive proofs.

\par\noindent\emph{Closure event induction and export refinement.}
\label{app:closure-proof}
\label{lem:export-refinement}
Registration stores candidates without advancing history, and locking fixes \(P_w, R_w\) before preparation. Atomic commit verifies actual heads and quota, persisting successor states and signed decisions sequentially. Role application is monotone, advancing status from pending to applied at most once. In export, each role verifies projections \(\pi_{\rm reg}(L_{S,w})=P_w\) and \(\pi_{\rm reg}(M_{S,w})=R_w\) against the locked graph, durably reserving \(\mathbf a_{S,w}\) before sending its share. \begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:related-crs-extraction}]
Let the generic adversary output an accepted proof \((A, B, C)\in\mathbb G_1\times\mathbb G_2\times\mathbb G_1\) under target key \(r\in\{\mathrm{gen},\mathrm{trans}\}\) (in phase-one setup \(\mathcal F_0\) with trapdoors \(X,\alpha,\beta,\Delta_r\), vanishing polynomial \(Z_r(X)=X^{n_r}-1\), and auxiliary setup \(\mathcal F_1\) on \((X',\alpha',\beta')\)) for instance \(\phi\). With \(Q_k(\phi)=\sum_{m=0}^{\ell_k}\phi_m(\beta_k u_{k,m}+\alpha_k v_{k,m}+w_{k,m})\) (\(\phi_0=1\)), each fresh source \(i\) under key \(r_i\) registers independent uniform \((\mu_i,\nu_i)\in\mathbb F_{p_{\rm proof}}^2\) and \(S_i=(\mu_i\nu_i-\alpha_{r_i}\beta_{r_i}-Q_{r_i}(\phi_i))/\Delta_{r_i}\) (with affine truncation \(S(3p_{\rm proof}-2)/p_{\rm proof}^2\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:groth-budget}). Over the complete typed handle basis, \(A = A_0 + \sum_i a_i \mu_i + \sum_i b_i S_i\), \(B = B_0 + \sum_j z_j \nu_j\), and \(C = C_0 + \sum_i \hat a_i \mu_i + \sum_i \hat b_i S_i\). Here \(A_0=\tilde a\alpha+\tilde b\beta+P+\sum_k c_k\Delta_k+\sum_k E_k/\Delta_k+A_{\rm aux}\) spans \(\mathbb G_1\) setup and imported handles (with \(\tilde a,\tilde b,P,c_k\in\mathbb F_{p_{\rm proof}}[X,Z]\), \(A_{\rm aux}\) in \(\mathcal F_1\), and \(E_k=\beta_k U_{k,e}+\alpha_k V_{k,e}+W_{k,e}+K_k\) combining private wires \(e\) and \(H\)-span numerator \(K_k\)), \(C_0\) expands identically with hatted coefficients \((\hat E_k,\hat e,\hat K_k)\), and \(B_0=\tilde h\alpha+\tilde f\beta+P_B+\sum_k g_k\Delta_k+B_{\rm aux}\) spans \(\Delta^{-1}\)-free \(\mathbb G_2\) handles. Outside collision bound \(g_{\rm coll}\), verification enforces \(A B = \alpha\beta + Q_r(\phi) + \Delta_r C\) formally. Comparing \(\mu_i\nu_i\nu_j\) gives \(b_i z_j = 0\) for all \(i,j\). If any \(b_{i_0}\neq 0\), then all \(z_j=0\), and \(\mu_i\nu_i\) gives \(b_i B_0 = \hat b_i \Delta_r\), so \(B_0 = \lambda\Delta_r\) and \(\hat b_i = \lambda b_i\) for \(\lambda=\hat b_{i_0}/b_{i_0}\). Canceling paired \(S_i\) terms leaves \(\Delta_r(\lambda A_0 - C_0) = \alpha\beta + Q_r(\phi)\), which is impossible since every \(E_k,\hat E_k\) is at most linear in \((\alpha_k,\beta_k)\) and cannot supply the \(\Delta_r\)-free \(\alpha\beta\) term, forcing \(b_i = 0\) for all \(i\).

With \(b_i=0\), comparing \(\mu_i\nu_j\) (\(i\neq j\)), \(\mu_i\nu_i\), \(\nu_j\), and \(\mu_i\) gives \(a_i z_j = 0\) (\(i\neq j\)), \(a_i z_i = \hat b_i (\Delta_r/\Delta_{r_i})\) (so independent \(\Delta_{r_i}\) force \(a_i z_i = \hat b_i = 0\) when \(r_i \neq r\), and \(\hat b_i = a_i z_i\) when \(r_i = r\)), \(z_j A_0 = 0\), and \(a_i B_0 = \hat a_i \Delta_r\). If all \(a_i z_i = 0\), any nonzero \(a_{i_0}\) yields \(B_0 = (\hat a_{i_0}/a_{i_0})\Delta_r\) and nonzero \(z_{j_0}\) yields \(A_0 = 0\), contradicting the \(\Delta_r\)-free \(\alpha\beta\) term. Hence either a unique target source \(i^*\) (\(r_{i^*}=r\)) has \(a_{i^*}z_{i^*}\neq 0\), or all \(a_i = z_j = \hat a_i = \hat b_i = 0\). In the first case, \(A_0 = 0\), \(B_0 = \tau\Delta_r\) with \(\hat a_{i^*} = \tau a_{i^*}\) (\(\hat a_i = 0\) for \(i\neq i^*\)), and the \(\Delta_r\)-free \(\alpha\beta\) term gives \(\hat b_{i^*} = a_{i^*}z_{i^*} = 1\), leaving \(Q_r(\phi) - Q_r(\phi_{i^*}) + \Delta_r C_0 = 0\). Thus \((A,B,C)\) reuses the exact registered target statement \((r,\text{relation},\phi_{i^*})\) even under re-randomization (\(\tau\neq 0\) or \(a_{i^*}\neq 1\)).

In the second case, \(A_0 B_0 = \alpha\beta + Q_r(\phi) + \Delta_r C_0\). Comparing \(\Delta\)-free quadratic terms in \((\alpha,\beta,\alpha',\beta')\) gives \(\tilde a \tilde h = 0\), \(\tilde b \tilde f = 0\), \(\tilde a \tilde f + \tilde b \tilde h = 1\), and vanishing \(\alpha',\beta'\) constants in \(A_{\rm aux},B_{\rm aux}\), fixing scalar unit \((\tilde a \tilde f = 1, \tilde b=\tilde h=0)\) or \((\tilde b \tilde h = 1, \tilde a=\tilde f=0)\) in \(\mathbb F_{p_{\rm proof}}^*\). \end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Predeclared multi-window privacy]
\label{thm:multi-window-privacy}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

The simulator maintains internal prefetch set \(A_{\rm queried}\) and externally exposed set \(A_{\rm visible}\), initialized to \(\emptyset\). (3)~\emph{Send or Crash.} Transmitting the share moves \(R_w\) to \(A_{\rm visible}\), releasing the aggregate via Eq.~\eqref{eq:simulated-honest-export}. Durable reservation replays identical snapshots on failure, bounding joint loss across \(k\) windows by \(e_X^{\rm MW}\).
\end{proof}
