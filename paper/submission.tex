\title{Enforcing Hidden-State Policies in Native Private Aggregation}
\author{}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Private heatmap aggregation over a predeclared client cohort requires authorizing hidden-state transitions without exposing client coordinates to the admission authority. Composing zero-knowledge state proofs with secret-shared aggregation leaves two semantic gaps: an individually valid share vector can encode a cell different from the state-authorized cell, and aggregators can agree on a batch that omits accepted reports or exposes a distinguishing subset. Stateful History-to-Payload Closure (SHTPC) binds hidden-state authorization, retained aggregator shares and complete-cohort first exposure. Within the one-hot histogram domain, a vector's linear index moment uniquely identifies its selected cell; aggregators sign masked-moment receipts over retained shares so admission verifies cell equality without encoding PRG seed expansion in the application circuit. Atomic admission serializes state transitions, and durable reservation locks the complete cohort before any honest aggregate share becomes decryptable. With honest services, SHTPC preserves accepted-history integrity across windows and crash recovery; against an active aggregator colluding with the Collector, it hides trajectories up to public metadata and equal declared complete-cohort histograms across single-release and predeclared multi-window executions. At 10,001 bins, the standalone binding circuit uses 5,779 constraints (versus 79,002,390 for in-circuit seed expansion), accelerates transition proving by 89.8--89.9$\times$ over Poseidon vector binding, and adds 1.44$\times$ batch latency (1.38$\times$ per session) over plaintext trusted generation in 100-client Janus runs while blocking cell mismatches and incomplete-cohort exposure.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A managed fleet with predeclared participants and coordinated sampling uses cohort heatmaps to detect congestion and dispatch vehicles without revealing individual driver trajectories~\cite{Popa2011PrivStats,Popa2009VPriv,Balasch2010PrETP}. Even when aggregation arithmetic is exact, semantic and membership mismatches distort operational decisions: redirecting reports away from their history-authorized cells fabricates hotspots---inflating a T-Drive modal cell count from 3 to 22--23 when 20 of 100 reports are substituted in offline replay (Section~\ref{sec:eval-real-traces})---whereas omitting accepted reports suppresses true demand (Figure~\ref{fig:gap}).

The application combines an admission authority that authorizes
transitions without reading client coordinates with two aggregation
roles that execute a native secret-shared aggregation protocol
\cite{Geoghegan2026DAP,Barnes2023VDAF08,Corrigan2017Prio}. A Collector consumes the resulting
cohort statistics. Private aggregation validates individual inputs
and combines them without revealing each input.
The remaining task is to connect the authorized transitions, retained
contributions and release membership to the same accepted history.

Existing protocols validate private inputs~\cite{Bell2023ACORN,Chowdhury2022EIFFeL,Rathee2023ELSA,Lycklama2023RoFL},
enforce inclusion or verify aggregates~\cite{Su2025SCIF,Acharya2025PEAR,Hahn2023VerSA},
and maintain private aggregate state~\cite{Ball2024Stateful}.
Suppose a state proof authorizes cell \(p\), while a native-valid report
encodes cell \(q\ne p\). If the checks examine these objects independently,
they accept the cross-spliced pair. A common identifier or signed body
associates the objects but does not establish semantic equality. A hiding
commitment binds its opening, but an additional relation connects that opening
to the native output actually retained. Because independent checks form a product
set of locally valid objects, they inevitably accept cross-spliced pairs
(formalized in Appendix~\ref{sec:composition-failures}).

State freshness and release membership create two further interfaces. A proof
for predecessor \(c\) remains valid after a competing successor commits, so
proof verification does not authorize the current transition. Separately, an
accepted report can be dropped on both receiver channels. The receivers then
agree on a valid but incomplete batch, as Figure~\ref{fig:gap} shows. Both
interfaces therefore depend on information beyond independent proof and
native-input checks.

Completeness also has a privacy deadline. Two legal histories can swap two
owners' positive cells while retaining the same public metadata and complete
heatmap. A subset containing only one of those owners has different aggregates.
Once the first honest share is decryptable, a corrupted aggregation role and
the Collector can distinguish the histories. A later publication refusal
cannot retract the exposure. Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure-separation}
formalizes this distinction. Accepted-history closure connects each admitted
claim to its retained contribution and legal state transition, and reconciles
the eligible set before first exposure.

Stateful History-to-Payload Closure (SHTPC) binds each hidden-state proof to
the share vector retained by the Aggregators without
reproducing native seed expansion inside the application circuit. Native
histogram validity restricts the reconstructed contribution to a one-hot
vector with a sentinel. Within this domain, its linear index moment identifies
the selected cell. Each role computes a masked receipt from its retained
output, and admission compares the paired receipts with the proof-bound masked
index. The application circuit is therefore independent of histogram width \(D\), while native
vector processing remains \(O(D)\).

The bridge introduces receipts and retry feedback that require joint analysis.
Authenticated source registration binds private processing to its registered
body, and joint simulation accounts for the resulting transcript. Atomic
admission advances the current history, while durable reservation fixes the
complete cohort before exposure. Figures~\ref{fig:architecture} and~\ref{fig:accepted-history-flow}
show their execution order. Integrity assumes honest services facing malicious clients and transport faults. Privacy permits
one actively corrupted aggregator colluding with the Collector. The
single-release and predeclared multi-window analyses require common metadata
and equal declared histograms for each permitted complete cohort.

SHTPC contributes an accepted-history contract, a compact native-output binding
and an analysis of their composition with private aggregation.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Masked-moment binding.} Native one-hot validity and masked
receipts from retained outputs bind a hidden-state proof to the actual
contribution without reproducing native seed expansion in the application
circuit. The standalone relation has 5,779 constraints at 10,001 bins, versus
79,002,390 for direct seed binding.

\item \emph{Accepted-history closure.} A security contract connects each
admitted claim, retained contribution and legal state transition to complete
eligible cohort membership at first exposure. A separation example shows why
publication refusal alone is too late. The construction maintains these
connections across retries and recovery under honest service execution.

\item \emph{Joint privacy of receipt feedback.} Masked receipts, rejection
feedback and retries are jointly simulatable with the native transcript
against an active aggregator colluding with the Collector. The exposure-time
interface uses no aggregate before the first honest share becomes
decryptable and one permitted
complete-cohort output at that boundary. Simulation distinguishes malicious
sources' declarations from their actual retained outputs. Native controls
exercise these interfaces, and component and lifecycle measurements report
their costs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Model and Security Contract}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Deployment and Trust Model}
\label{sec:threat}

Enrolled clients report under stable pseudonymous identities. An admission
authority verifies hidden state proofs and maintains accepted history without
receiving coordinate openings or role decryption keys. Two Aggregators, acting
as the standard Leader and Helper in the underlying DAP protocol~\cite{Geoghegan2026DAP}
(algebraically roles \(S\in\{A,B\}\), or corrupted \(c\) and honest \(h\) in privacy analysis),
prepare input shares and retain aggregation outputs. The Collector holds the aggregate-share
HPKE key, and its Decoder verifies release records and reconstructs the heatmap. The
authority-side Finalizer authenticates the frozen reference and its completion.
Its certificate joins that reference to both roles' matching attestations. A durable admission proxy
belongs to the same trust domain and delivers signed decisions. Each role
durably reserves its own share before export.

Integrity and privacy operate under distinct corruption models. For integrity, clients
and network transport are malicious, while admission, both Aggregators, Finalizer,
Collector and Decoder follow the protocol using serialized durable storage; this fits a managed fleet
enforcing admission and release rules without granting the authority plaintext coordinates.
For privacy, one Aggregator may be actively corrupted in collusion with the
Collector, and the adversary may additionally observe the honest authority's business records,
receipts, diagnostics and backups (excluding its signing key).
Because an active Aggregator can forge shares or abort, this corruption model targets
privacy rather than output integrity; a corrupted admission authority is outside both models.
Crucially, forged receipts cannot serve as a probing oracle. Admission acceptance for
an honest report depends solely on the corrupt role's own fixed original share and mask,
leaking no honest coordinates (Section~\ref{sec:joint-privacy}).

Privacy challenges use legal histories with common public structure \(\mu\),
including identities, task and key, windows, step and anchor references,
eligibility, contribution bits, packet rules and the delivery plan. Proofs,
ciphertexts, commitments, receipts, errors, aborts and retries are simulated
jointly. The authority has no aggregate interface. Definition~\ref{game:privacy} gives the deployed single-release contract,
with one visible cohort per task and key. Theorem~\ref{thm:multi-window-privacy}
extends the analysis to predeclared windows in the same task and key namespace.
Reports and retained outputs each belong to one window. Later windows can
reference earlier accepted states as predecessors or anchors.
Privacy bounds protocol leakage beyond the permitted histograms and \(\mu\).
Activity, contribution bits and cadence remain public, and exact outputs
can support inference from auxiliary knowledge.

All guarantees assume correctly sampled public parameters with unknown trapdoors, reviewed fixed-width
arithmetic, authenticated confidential registration and durable execution boundaries.
Enrollment authenticates pseudonymous identities rather than physical GPS locations or Sybil
resistance, continuity is enforced over authenticated slots rather than unverified elapsed time, and
authority equivocation lies outside this scope.

\subsection{Reports, Histories and Cohorts}
\label{sec:prelim-model}

Reports move through three distinct units of progress. A sampling slot assigns
a measurement to time, an accepted step counts a committed transition, and a
release window groups eligible reports. Rejected attempts do not advance the
accepted history.

Each report is projected and quantized under fixed public parameters, yielding
a hidden vector \(\mathbf{x}_r\). A \(W\times H\) grid with cell width \(s\)
selects
\begin{equation}
  p_r=\mathsf{Grid}(\mathbf{x}_r)
  =W\Bigl\lfloor\frac{y_r}{s}\Bigr\rfloor+\Bigl\lfloor\frac{x_r}{s}\Bigr\rfloor,
  \label{eq:canonical-grid}
\end{equation}
where \(D=WH\). The evaluated profile uses \(s=100\) and \(W=H=100\).
The replay projection, rounding and boundary rules appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:policy}.

All record maps are keyed by report identifier, tracking progress across the three lifecycle stages in Table~\ref{tab:record-types}.
First, during \emph{registration and locking}, clients register report cores before preparation feedback, forming the complete registered history map \(P_w\) and its eligible projection \(R_w\). Warmups enter log \(P_w\) to advance state counters but are excluded from eligible map \(R_w\), whereas eligible zero-bit records (\(b_r=0\)) remain in \(R_w\) despite adding no mass. Let \(I_w=\operatorname{dom}(R_w)\); honest report identifiers form \(I_{\rm hon}\), partitioned as \(I_w^{\rm hon}=I_w\cap I_{\rm hon}\) and \(I_w^{\rm adv}=I_w\setminus I_{\rm hon}\). Projection \(\pi_{\rm reg}\) preserves registered fields while removing later receipts, commit decisions, and retained-output links; map equality compares keys and values. The concurrent log is \(\mathcal L\), and \(H\) is a legal sequential history.
Second, during \emph{admission commitment}, reports that pass zero-knowledge transition proofs, masked receipts, and atomic history checks are committed, yielding committed log \(L_w\) and eligible map \(\mathcal A_w\).
Third, during \emph{role application}, roles \(S\in\{A,B\}\) process committed decisions into applied maps \(L_{S,w}\) and \(M_{S,w}\). For each applied report \(r\), role \(S\) retains share \(v_{S,r}\in\mathbb F_q^{D+1}\) with sentinel; its reserved sum \(\mathbf a_{S,w}\) yields the reconstructed \(D\)-cell heatmap \(\hat{\mathbf h}_w\) upon decoding.

For report \(r\), the common admitted object is
\begin{equation}
  A_r=(\mathsf{rid}_r,\eta_r,\mathsf{stmt}_r,\mathsf{pay}_r,
       c_{r-1},c_r,w,b_r).
  \label{eq:admission-object}
\end{equation}
The context \(\eta_r\) binds identity, window and policy. The remaining fields
identify the proof statement, bound channel packages, predecessor, successor
and contribution bit. A complete signed body, two retained-output receipts and
one commit decision authenticate this object in the native realization
(Appendix~\ref{app:native-theory}).

Strict release produces the complete eligible cohort or refuses, serving applications
that require coordinated sampling and can tolerate delayed batch release. Execution separates
failures across three distinct phases:
(1)~\emph{Pre-lock registration failure}, where a client fails to submit a complete signed
body or assign a required contiguous slot before the registration lock boundary;
(2)~\emph{Post-registration validation stall}, where an installed body contains an invalid
proof, references an uncommitted anchor, or violates quota; and
(3)~\emph{Post-commit transport failure}, where network disconnection occurs after an admission
decision is durably committed.
Critically, once a client's complete request is registered and committed, subsequent vehicle
disconnection does not halt cohort processing: server-side durable redelivery and idempotent retry
continue role application and export autonomously without requiring continuous client online presence.
A ``permanently missing member'' specifically denotes a client failing Phase~(1) registration or
Phase~(2) proof validation. If any member is permanently missing, release is strictly refused;
SHTPC preserves complete-cohort integrity and prohibits dynamic member dropouts or publishing over
the receiver intersection set. Table~\ref{tab:lifecycle} gives its window lifecycle.

\subsection{Accepted-History Closure}
\label{sec:boundaries}
\label{sec:prelim-goals}
\label{sec:composition-summary}

Four obligations share \(A_r\). P1 connects the proved claim to the consumed
package by
\begin{equation}
\mathsf{Contrib}(\mathsf{pay}_{r,\mathrm{consumed}})
  =b_r\mathbf e_{p_r}.
  \label{eq:p1}
\end{equation}
P2 requires both roles to consume channels of that same object. P3 gives each
accepted object one atomic transition with its quota charge,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{State}_{r-1}\rightarrow\mathrm{State}_{r}.
  \label{eq:p3}
\end{equation}
P4 requires a successful release to contain exactly the eligible committed
records and the complete locked reference,
\begin{equation}
  \pi_{\rm reg}(\mathcal A_w)=R_w.
  \label{eq:p4}
\end{equation}
These obligations organize the successful-trace and exposure-prefix
definitions below. Theorem~\ref{thm:main-semantic-closure} establishes their
composition under the stated integrity premises.

\begin{definition}[Legal sequential history]
\label{def:legal-history}
A sequence \(H=(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\) with unique identifiers is legal if replay
from enrolled genesis enables every transition. Each step has an authorized
owner and context, names the current head and policy-selected accepted anchor,
and satisfies the application relation on opened states. It atomically appends
the successor, advances the head and accepted-step counter, and charges quota
without changing other owners. Rejected and uncommitted attempts add no step.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[History refinement]
\label{def:history-refinement}
For a finite prefix, \(\mathcal L\preceq H\) when committed admission
operations bijectively map to a legal \(H\), preserving every \(A_r\) field
and the order of linearization points between invocation and response.
Committed pending operations are completed at the prefix end, while
uncommitted attempts are discarded. Retries of one durable decision represent
one operation, and distinct committed effects remain distinct.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Successful-trace closure]
\label{def:trace-closure}
Fix a transition policy, additive contribution map \(r\mapsto m_r\) and
eligibility rule. A successful release has closure if some legal \(H\)
satisfies \(\mathcal L\preceq H\). Let \(E_w(H)\) be the set of records in
the complete accepted history \(H\) that belong to window \(w\) and satisfy
the eligibility rule. Their identifiers equal \(\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal A_w)\),
and each identifies one common admitted object across proof, packages,
transition and certificate. Each role's application count \(N_{S,w}(r)\) satisfies
\(N_{S,w}(r)=\mathbf1[r\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal A_w)]\), and its reserved sum uses exactly
those retained outputs. The pre-noise output is
\(\sum_{r\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal A_w)}m_r\). For heatmaps,
\(m_r=b_r\mathbf e_{\mathsf{Grid}(\mathbf x_r)}\). Eligibility is independent
of bit value.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Exposure-prefix safety]
\label{def:exposure-prefix}
Every decryptable honest share comes from a prior durable reservation
satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \pi_{\rm reg}(L_{S,w})=P_w,\qquad
 \pi_{\rm reg}(M_{S,w})=R_w=\pi_{\rm reg}(\mathcal A_w),\\
 \mathbf a_{S,w}=\sum_{r\in I_w}v_{S,r}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:export-safety}
\end{equation}
Retries preserve its membership and plaintext. Successful-trace closure also
requires correct final reconstruction.
\end{definition}

\section{SHTPC Design}
\label{sec:system}

SHTPC realizes P1--P4 by connecting hidden-state authorization to
retained aggregation outputs and complete first exposure.
\subsection{Design Overview}
\label{sec:architecture-choice}
\label{sec:overview}

Anchor Distance Window Continuity (ADWC) bounds each hidden move relative to
the last accepted position and a policy-selected historical anchor. The client proves ADWC and grid
membership over hidden states. Native validity and masked-moment receipts
connect the proved cell to the outputs retained by the aggregation roles.
Admission checks these receipts and authorizes their use against the current
accepted history. Figure~\ref{fig:accepted-history-flow} shows how this
connection reaches a complete release.

\subsection{End-to-End Workflow}
Enrollment fixes owner keys, a proved hidden genesis and the task configuration.
Each window then proceeds through five steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Lock.} Clients register complete signed bodies with admission. The
authority verifies the candidate graph and locks \(P_w\) and \(R_w\) before
private preparation or receipt feedback.
\item \emph{Retain.} Both roles install the lock. A permit authorizes preparation
against the current head and anchor. Each role prepares its input, retains its
native output and signs a masked-moment receipt.
\item \emph{Commit.} Admission checks both receipts and their masked-moment
equality. A transaction rechecks the actual head, anchor and quota, then stores
the successor, history and signed decision together.
\item \emph{Apply.} A durable proxy delivers the decision to each role. Each
role applies it once and updates its retained history and eligible map. Warmups
advance history without entering the eligible map.
\item \emph{Reserve.} Each role checks its applied records against the frozen
reference and verifies the retained-output links. It durably reserves the complete
sum and selector before sending its share. Retries use the same snapshot.
\end{enumerate}
Preparation creates a retained output, while admission authorizes its use in
aggregation.

\subsection{Binding Hidden State to Native Outputs}
\label{sec:native-adaptor}

P1 binds the state-authorized cell to the retained native output without encoding
native PRG seed expansion inside the zero-knowledge circuit. Under native private
aggregation, the underlying verifiable aggregation layer guarantees one-hot validity of
the reconstructed vector (\cite[Section~7.4.4]{Barnes2023VDAF08}): for roles $A$ and $B$
retaining shares $v_A, v_B \in \mathbb F_q^{D+1}$, the reconstructed output satisfies
$v = v_A + v_B = \mathbf e_y$ for a unique unit coordinate $y \in [0, D]$.

Over this one-hot domain, the linear index moment $\sum_{j=0}^D j v[j] = y$ uniquely
identifies the selected index $y$ (for example, in a three-cell vector $(0,0,1)$,
$0\cdot0+1\cdot0+2\cdot1=2$). In native field $\mathbb F_q$ with $D < q$, let a state-derived
primary $p < D$ and public bit $b$ authorize target index $x = bp + (1-b)D$, with sentinel $D$.
The client samples private uniform masks $r_A, r_B \in \mathbb F_q$, commits to their openings,
and encrypts $r_S$ for role $S \in \{A, B\}$. The ZK proof binds $x$ and the masks to
the public target $T$:
\begin{equation}
 T = x + r_A + r_B \pmod q.
 \label{eq:native-target}
\end{equation}
Each correct role computes a local masked moment from its retained output:
\begin{equation}
 m_S = \sum_{j=0}^{D} j v_S[j] + r_S \pmod q.
 \label{eq:native-moment}
\end{equation}
Because moments are linear, summing the two receipts causes the masks to cancel:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 m_A + m_B &= \sum_{j=0}^D j (v_A[j] + v_B[j]) + r_A + r_B \\
           &= y + r_A + r_B \pmod q.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:moment-cancellation}
\end{equation}
Consequently, checking $m_A + m_B = T$ directly tests $y = x \pmod q$. Lemma~\ref{lem:native-moment-fidelity}
shows that acceptance forces the reconstructed contribution to equal $b\mathbf e_p + (1-b)\mathbf e_D$,
recovering $b\mathbf e_p$ upon sentinel removal. Table~\ref{tab:binding-designs} compares
this approach against generic vector binding and direct seed binding.

\noindent\textbf{Transition circuit relation (ADWC and grid).}
The public statement binds the state commitments, report context and masked
index. Its witness opens the state, ticket, mask and receipt commitments.
The relation binds the payload witness's cell and
remainder to the successor and enforces
\(p=\mathsf{Grid}(\mathbf x_r)\),
\(\|\mathbf x_r-\mathbf x_{\rm prev}\|^2\le\tau_u^2\Delta t_t^2\) and
\(\|\mathbf x_r-\mathbf a_t\|^2\le
\min\{\tau_u^2(\Delta T_t^{(K)})^2,c_{\rm policy}^2\}\).
Here \(\mathbf a_t\) is the policy-selected accepted anchor, \(\tau_u\) is the
speed tier and \(c_{\rm policy}\) is the anchor cap. Admission checks that the
referenced predecessor and anchor are the actual accepted ones. The same grid
and distance constraints apply when \(b=0\). Appendix~\ref{app:policy} gives
the parameters, and the artifact's fixed transition circuit gives the ordered
public-signal interface (Section~\ref{sec:artifact}).

\noindent\textbf{Cross-field canonical encoding (\(\mathbb F_q\) vs.\ \(p_{\rm proof}\)).}
To bridge native field \(\mathbb F_q\) and proof scalar field \(p_{\rm proof}\),
canonical scalars enforce \(x+r_A+r_B=T+\chi q\) with carry \(\chi\in\{0,1,2\}\) over integers,
preventing wrap-around between disparate field moduli (Appendix~\ref{app:native-interface}).
State commitments retain birth domains and references, and admission verifies the
masked-moment sum without receiving coordinate openings or Aggregator decryption keys.

\noindent\textbf{Complexity and idempotent preparation.}
Because moment checking operates on scalars while share evaluation scans \(D+1\) buckets,
the application circuit size is independent of histogram width \(D\), whereas native vector
processing remains \(O(D)\). Separating share preparation from accumulation ensures that each
signed receipt authenticates the package context and retained share, with exact retries
replaying the same output and receipt.

The compact binding relation relies on linear injectivity over the one-hot domain: a single
first-order index moment is injective specifically on unit vectors $\mathbf e_y$, but is not
injective on general vectors (for instance, $(0, 2, 0)$ and $(1, 0, 1)$ yield the identical
moment 2). Extending this moment-cancellation bridge to other stateful applications requires
domain-appropriate mappings: multi-dimensional spatial or categorical outputs may require
multiple higher-order moments or component-wise binding, and weighted contributions require
jointly binding weights and indices (e.g., certifying both $\sum w_j$ and $\sum j w_j$).
Stateful applications such as bounded cumulative usage can adopt this approach whenever
their reconstructed output domains admit an injective moment relation.

\subsection{Protecting the Feedback Interface}
\label{sec:origin-feedback}
\label{sec:cross-role}

Encryption does not authenticate an input's owner. An attacker can transplant
an honest ciphertext and substitute a known mask, causing an honest receipt to
reveal the cell before admission rejects the report. This feedback arises from
the new bridge even when the proof and native protocol are secure in isolation.

SHTPC allocates an owner identifier to one complete signed body and registers
it before private processing. Each ingress authenticates the consumed metadata,
ciphertexts, sidecars, installed lock and permit against that body. Both
retained sessions and receipts therefore refer to the same admitted object,
realizing P2. Source registration, origin locking, and fixed original shares prevent
an active adversary from splicing components across reports or adapting receipts to
probe honest state. Because the original share and mask are permanently locked to
the registered body, and retry/abort states are strictly serialized, adaptive feedback
cannot alter the receipt passing condition.

Recovery retains the same source and native state. The Helper journals its
first protected input and response before sending. Identical retries replay
those bytes, while changed inputs or new jobs cannot reopen that branch. The
Leader's lease and transaction fence permit at most one committed transition.
Uncommitted computation can receive new peer feedback, which the joint privacy
analysis includes explicitly.

\subsection{Atomic History Authorization}
\label{sec:admission}

A transition proof can remain valid after another successor commits. Admission
therefore verifies the proof before the transaction, then atomically rechecks
the actual head, anchor and quota and stores the successor, history and signed
decision. Both retained-output checks
precede this linearization point. Preparation, admission and role application
use separate databases connected by that decision and its output links.

For stable identity \(u\), task and window \(w\), durable quota
\(q_{u,w}\in\{0,1\}\) is shared across credential epochs. With accepted round
\(t\) and \(N_{\rm warm}=\max(K,2)\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_t&=b_t\mathbf1[t>N_{\rm warm}],\\
q_{u,w}+\gamma_t&\le1,\qquad
q_{u,w}\gets q_{u,w}+\gamma_t.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:window-quota}
\end{equation}
Commit induction gives
\begin{equation}
q_{u,w}=\sum_{\substack{r\in\mathcal A_w\\\mathsf{uid}(r)=u}} b_r\le1.
\label{eq:quota-invariant}
\end{equation}
Warmups remain in the transition log without charge. Eligible zero-bit updates
remain members and can advance state after quota is spent.

History is keyed by stable \((\mathsf{task},\mathsf{uid})\), with credential,
revision, window, receipt and budget checks inside the transaction. Credential
epochs and storage revisions do not replace accepted-step counters or sampling
slots. The proxy
queues exact decisions durably before delivery. Role databases apply each
decision once, including out-of-order delivery. Retries return that decision,
and credential rotation preserves history and spent quota.

\subsection{Complete Exposure and Recovery}
\label{sec:finalization}
\label{sec:strict-choice}
P4 fixes membership before preparation feedback can influence it. Admission
locks the verified candidate graph and its eligible projection at the public
registration boundary. The graph includes warmups and eligible zeros together
with their predecessor and anchor references. Preparation errors and receipts cannot select a smaller cohort or replace
a locked body. Export by role \(S\) requires
\begin{equation}
 \pi_{\rm reg}(L_{S,w})=P_w,\qquad \pi_{\rm reg}(M_{S,w})=R_w.
 \label{eq:final-report-set}
\end{equation}
These equalities compare the frozen registration cores, including context and
body. The role separately checks each actual retained-output link against its
authenticated receipt and commit decision.

In one export transaction, the role verifies Eq.~\eqref{eq:final-report-set} and reserves the
selector, record map, retained-output links, and exact cohort sum
(\label{sec:profiles}\label{sec:dp}under the strict profile) before sending its share.
Because role application is monotone (\(0 \to 1\)), this atomic check refines
Definition~\ref{def:trace-closure} by ensuring all locked members are applied before exposure (Appendix~\ref{boundary:appendix}).
Persisting the resulting snapshot before transmission ensures that re-exports idempotently
re-emit the identical complete sum without altering membership. Warmup completion is checked
through \(L_{S,w}\), and eligible zeros remain in \(M_{S,w}\); numerical equality alone cannot
establish this membership because an omitted eligible zero has no displayed mass.
Responses and re-encrypted retries
read the same durable snapshot, so recovery cannot change its plaintext or
membership. The Finalizer authenticates the frozen reference and both roles'
matching attestations. The Decoder checks that certificate and the reserved
records before reconstruction.

After locking, any member failing registration or validation holds or aborts the
cohort without silent exclusion or intersection release (Section~\ref{sec:prelim-model}).
Conversely, committed decisions survive client disconnection via autonomous
server-side redelivery without re-upload. Collection failure preserves the
first exposure and reserved snapshot. Pre-exposure abort retains partial commits,
heads and quota for continuation as in Table~\ref{tab:lifecycle}.
For example, owners left at steps 2 and 1 resume at 3 and 2 with new bodies
referencing those heads. An incompatible schedule cannot lock. Configuration
remains bound to the database. Theorem~\ref{thm:multi-window-privacy} gives
the privacy conditions for predeclared windows.

\label{prop:native-post-admission-progress}
For a finite fully committed lock, eventual service and channel recovery with
fair retries and no deadline stop gives complete release. Each successful apply
removes one of at most \(2|P_w|\) pending role-record pairs. Permanent client refusal and incomplete pre-lock registration fall outside
these progress conditions. Admission caps bound accepted work and exact retries
consume no new slot. The native profile uses \(B_u=16\) per owner and window.

\begin{definition}[Closure game]
\label{game:closure}
The challenger initializes honest service keys, enrolled identities and genesis
state. A bounded PPT adversary controls malicious clients and delivery. It
submits packages, observes responses, replays, replaces, reorders or suppresses
messages, and invokes admission and window closure. Honest signing keys and
committed service state remain outside its control. The challenger records the
locked \(P_w,R_w\), invocation and response events, committed objects and
receiver application counts. The
adversary wins if the Decoder accepts a release for which no legal
linearization exists, or any membership, application-count, common-object
or reconstruction condition of Definition~\ref{def:trace-closure} fails,
or if a
native role exports a share that violates Eq.~\eqref{eq:export-safety}.
Refusal without an unsafe export is not a win. The game fixes the strict
profile. Write
\(\operatorname{Adv}_{\rm closure}(\mathcal B)=\Pr[\mathsf{Win}]\).
Serializable durable state and honest service execution are premises of this game.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Native moment fidelity]
\label{lem:native-moment-fidelity}
Assume sound relation certification, commitment binding, authenticated common
body identity, correct receivers and native validity. Let the certified
canonical coordinate be \(\mathbf x_r\), with \(p=\mathsf{Grid}(\mathbf x_r)<D<q\)
and \(b\in\{0,1\}\). Let \(x=bp+(1-b)D\), with canonical
\(r_A,r_B,T\in[0,q-1]\), and \(F(v)=\sum_{j=0}^{D}jv[j]\). Suppose the
retained outputs satisfy \(v_A+v_B=\mathbf e_y\) and the receipts satisfy
\(m_S=F(v_S)+r_S\).
If certification fixes \(T=x+r_A+r_B\pmod q\) and admission checks
\(m_A+m_B=T\), the reconstructed contribution after sentinel removal is
\(b\mathbf e_p\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Opening checks fix the certified masks. Linearity gives
\(y=F(v_A+v_B)=T-r_A-r_B=x\pmod q\).
Since \(x,y\in[0,D]\) and \(D<q\), this is integer equality, and
removing sentinel \(D\) yields \(b\mathbf e_p\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[First-exposure separation]
\label{prop:exposure-separation}
Under the share-observation interface of Section~\ref{sec:threat}, suppose
two metadata-compatible admissible histories have the same aggregate
on a full accepted set \(\mathcal C\), but different aggregates on a permitted native
batch \(R\subsetneq\mathcal C\). Native validity, atomic admission and exact-set
checking at final publication do not imply exposure-prefix safety or privacy
relative to the full aggregate. This holds even for a wrapper that successfully
publishes \(\mathcal C\) on complete delivery.
\end{proposition}
The separating execution delivers complementary shares of \(R\) to the
corrupted role and Collector, then refuses publication because \(R\ne\mathcal C\).
Appendix~\ref{app:exposure-separation} constructs the two legal histories.
The exposure distinguishes the histories despite the later publication refusal.

The analysis uses typed generic bilinear groups without a target-to-source

trapdoors, independent latent variables and ceremonies with at least one

\begin{lemma}[Related-setup extraction]
\label{lem:related-crs-extraction}
Under these source rules, every accepted proof yields a witness or names a
registered source with identical key, relation and ordered public signals,
except with probability at most \(g\).
Appendix~\ref{app:primitive-reductions} gives its finite budget.
\end{lemma}

The joint source representation tracks real and simulated proofs under

divisibility forces the proof elements to encode a valid assignment for the target relation,
extracting the witness; unequal formal expressions are charged to the generic group collision bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Accepted-history composition at first exposure]
\label{thm:main-semantic-closure}
In Definition~\ref{game:closure} under Section~\ref{sec:threat}, let \(B_C\) bound
the combined probability of whole-execution proof extraction, commitment binding,
channel-package authentication, digest collision, and origin guessing failures,
and let \(v_N\) bound native validity failure. Correct serialized durable execution
is a premise. For the verification-key tag in \(v_N\), the integrity instantiation
adopts the SHA256 one-wayness assumption on uniform 128-bit keys with resource-bounded
advantage \(a_{\rm OW128}\). Then
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Adv}_{\rm closure}\le\min\{1,B_C+v_N\}.
 \label{eq:primitive-bounds}
\end{equation}
Concrete parameterizations, query budgets, and algebraic expansions for \(B_C\)
and \(v_N\) appear in Appendix~\ref{app:primitive-reductions}.
Outside those failure events, every first export and retry satisfies
Eq.~\eqref{eq:export-safety} with one immutable snapshot.
The accepted log refines a legal ADWC history under Definitions~\ref{def:legal-history}
and~\ref{def:history-refinement}. Every successful strict release satisfies
Definition~\ref{def:trace-closure} and reconstructs
\begin{equation}
 \hat{\mathbf h}_w=\sum_{r\in I_w}b_r\mathbf e_{\mathsf{Grid}(\mathbf x_r)}.
 \label{eq:closure-release}
\end{equation}
Each record has one common admission object across its proof, consumed
packages, committed transition, and release certificate. These invariants
persist through recovery and collection failure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Under the source rules of Lemma~\ref{lem:related-crs-extraction}, honest
same-source reuse retains its registered semantics, malicious sources use known
or extracted witnesses, and foreign namespaces or contexts are rejected.
Commitment binding fixes the predecessor and anchor. Native validity and Lemma~\ref{lem:native-moment-fidelity}
bind its retained output to the proved cell. Induction over registration,
lock, permit, commit and apply preserves the fixed candidate maps and a legal
accepted history. The export transaction checks full completion and fixes
the share before sending. Recovery preserves that transaction's snapshot. The roles apply the durable
certificate idempotently without a cross-database atomic commit. After a
correct freeze, complete membership is stable under monotone application, so
an earlier incomplete snapshot can refuse but cannot authorize a smaller set.
Consequently every exposure prefix satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:export-safety},
and reconstruction gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:closure-release}.
Appendix~\ref{app:closure-proof} gives the event cases.
\end{proof}

\label{lem:dp-input-correctness}

privacy or output policy.

Bounding verification-key guessing from the public 96-bit tag in \(v_N\) requires

\subsection{Joint Privacy with Feedback}

\begin{definition}[Single-cohort privacy experiment]
\label{game:privacy}
Fix a finite public resource budget before setup. Sample a correct setup and
fix the task, profile, keys and static corruption set before any honest
measurement. The budget covers honest and
adversarial work, including warmups, zero-bit reports, failed and unprepared
reports, verification, oracle calls, bytes and retries. Execution stops at the
budget. The environment supplies two complete legal histories \(H_0,H_1\) for
honest sources with the same public skeleton \(\mu\). It fixes each source's
challenge inputs before its first report, while the adversary can adaptively
request reports from those histories. Honest reports reach admission over an
independent authenticated confidential channel according to the input-ready
plan. Local witness and proof generation time is outside the online view.

For \(X\in\{A,P,P{+}A\}\), the challenger selects a uniform hidden bit \(d\), runs
the protocol on \(H_d\), and interacts with a bounded PPT adversary
\(\mathcal B\) with the following view. In \(A\),
the correct authority's view includes its business records, receipts,
diagnostics, database and backups, and its ordinary authorization secrets.
It has no role decryption keys or aggregate output. The observer may compute
offline with its keys but cannot use them to issue extra authority signatures,
change the configuration or bypass the state machine. In \(P\), one actively corrupted
Aggregator and the Collector share their state, decrypted inputs, peer
messages and actual available Collector results. The corrupted role can
fabricate messages and shares or abort. In \(P{+}A\), that observer also sees
the correct authority's business view, without its authorization secret or
malicious issuance. The adversary can control corrupted owners and schedule,
delay, replay or drop messages within the model. Each view includes its
visible proofs, ciphertexts, commitments, responses, refusals, retries, aborts
and modeled online times. These outcomes are not assumed to be part of \(\mu\).

For each record, let \(y_i^{(d)}=b_i p_i^{(d)}+(1-b_i)D\) denote its
declared native index, where \(p_i^{(d)}\) is its declared primary cell. For
each allowed complete cohort \(R_w\), the ideal interface returns the
declared complete-cohort histogram \(\mathcal O_d(R_w)=\sum_{i\in I_w}
\mathbf e_{y_i^{(d)}}\), including sentinel \(D\). Write \(\mathcal O(R_w)\)
when the world is clear; because a corrupted role or source can alter shares, \(\mathcal O_d(R_w)\)
sums declared indices rather than the Collector's reconstructed output. The \(A\)
simulator receives no aggregate query and its two histories need not have
equal histograms. The \(P\) and \(P{+}A\) histories have equal declared complete-cohort histograms for
every allowed complete cohort. Their simulators may query that full cohort
once at the boundary where the first honest share becomes decryptable, even if
collection later fails. This exposure-time interface returns no output
before that boundary. Retries reuse the reserved plaintext without another
query. No subset query is permitted. The game covers one visible selector
for the fixed task and key.

The adversary outputs a bit in \(\mathsf{Exp}_X^d\). Its distinguishing
advantage is \(\operatorname{Adv}_X(\mathcal B)=
|\Pr[\mathsf{Exp}_X^1(\mathcal B)=1]-
\Pr[\mathsf{Exp}_X^0(\mathcal B)=1]|\).
\end{definition}

Theorem~\ref{thm:multi-window-privacy} uses a private-prefetch variant of this
interface. After durable reservation of a complete cohort, send preparation
can query its output before the first honest share becomes decryptable. The answer
remains private if preparation aborts without sending. The proof couples
this early evaluation with exposure-time evaluation, preserving observable
prefixes, including executions that never expose a share. Recovery retains
the same cohort and cached answer, maintaining indistinguishability across aborted runs.

\begin{theorem}[Joint privacy of admission and aggregation views]
\label{thm:joint-privacy-main}
Fix the privacy experiment of Definition~\ref{game:privacy} with static corruption,
the strict profile, and the source and programmable-oracle models in
Appendix~\ref{app:primitive-reductions}. The authority view is simulatable without honest coordinates or an aggregate.
The aggregation view, including any shared authority business view, is simulatable
from the observer's inputs, \(\mu\) and the permitted complete-cohort histogram. The distinguishing advantage of each observer \(X\) is at most
\(\operatorname{Adv}_X\le\min\{1,2e_X\}\), where \(e_X\) composes the
primitive bounds above, the hidden HPKE encapsulations and the native
stream simulation. Theorem~\ref{thm:native-single-batch-privacy} gives the
concrete form and a hybrid outline.
\end{theorem}

The masked receipts provide the key link. Let \(c\) be the actively corrupted role
and \(h\) the honest role. For an honest source report, the honest role computes
\(m_h = F(v_h^{\rm orig}) + r_h\) over its retained share, where \(F(v) = \sum_j j v[j]\).
Admission accepts paired receipts \((m_h, z)\) if and only if \(m_h + z = T\). Because
\(T = x + r_h + r_c \pmod q\) and native one-hot validity guarantees \(F(v_h^{\rm orig}) + F(v_c^{\rm orig}) = x\),
an arbitrary corrupt receipt \(z\) passes admission if and only if
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 z &= T - m_h = (x + r_h + r_c) - (F(v_h^{\rm orig}) + r_h) \\
   &= F(v_c^{\rm orig}) + r_c \pmod q.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:receipt-simulation}
\end{equation}
Thus, whether \(z\) passes depends entirely on \(F(v_c^{\rm orig}) + r_c\)---quantities determined
strictly by the corrupt role's own input share and mask.

Crucially, this refusal predicate establishes that malicious feedback cannot serve as a probing oracle
under the joint distribution \((v_c^{\rm orig}, r_c, T, m_h, \mathsf{status})\). This invariance is not
a heuristic appeal to the marginal uniformity of \(r_h\). Rather, authenticated source registration
binds the complete client package prior to role processing, locking \(v_c^{\rm orig}\) and \(r_c\)
to the immutable body. Idempotent retries and linearized commits evaluate against
this same fixed predicate. Because \(x\), \(v_h^{\rm orig}\), and \(r_h\) cancel out
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:receipt-simulation}, admission outcomes carry zero mutual information
with honest trajectories.

For the simulator, uniform \(T\) determines the coupled honest receipt in Eq.~\eqref{eq:receipt-simulation}
prior to learning the cell. For the authority alone, uniform \(T, U\) give \((m_A, m_B) = (U, T-U)\) without
an aggregate query. These identities join the receipt simulation to the native preparation transcript and
the same causal history state.

The simulator first classifies each request by its registered body and exact
statement, including the key namespace and ordered public signals. Honest
same-source reuse preserves registered business semantics and its native handle.
Malicious sources use known or extracted witnesses. Foreign namespaces or
contexts are rejected. A changed honest body under the same statement
requires a digest collision or an unauthorized signature. For a fresh
statement, extraction supplies the committed openings, not the ciphertext
plaintext. A ciphertext distinct from the protected challenge tuple is handled
by legitimate decryption and native execution. This routing permits malicious
sources without providing an oracle for an honest source's challenge.

For an honest source, the coupled receipt in Eq.~\eqref{eq:receipt-simulation}
uses the same native share as the preparation transcript. For a malicious
source, let \(y_j\) be its extracted or known claimed index and \(w_{h,j}\)
the actual successful output of the honest role. The simulator does not assume
that these outputs satisfy the honest-source identity. When reservation permits
first exposure, the single complete-cohort query \(\mathcal O(R_w)\) gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 V_{h,R_w}={}&\mathcal O(R_w)-\sum_{j\in I_w^{\rm adv}}\mathbf e_{y_j}\\
 &-\sum_{i\in I_w^{\rm hon}}v_{c,i}^{\rm orig}
 +\sum_{j\in I_w^{\rm adv}}w_{h,j}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:simulated-honest-export}
\end{equation}
Here \(I_w^{\rm hon}\) and \(I_w^{\rm adv}\) partition the eligible report identifiers. Subtracting the known or extracted malicious-source declarations
leaves the honest-source sum. For each honest source,
\(v_{h,i}=\mathbf e_{y_i}-v_{c,i}^{\rm orig}\). Subtracting the original
corrupt-role shares therefore gives their honest-role aggregate over honest reports. Adding the
actual honest-role outputs \(w_{h,j}\) for malicious sources completes the
joint view without subset queries or assuming that those sources follow their declarations.
Retries reuse the reserved plaintext. The authority-only simulator uses the zero-query receipt law above.

budgets for the joint simulation.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work-summary}

To position SHTPC within the literature, related private aggregation systems classify along three core dimensions:
(1)~\emph{Hidden-state history}: zero-knowledge verification of sequential per-identity state transitions (e.g., speed and anchor bounds);
(2)~\emph{\(O(1)\) native-output binding}: constant-size connection between client ZK claims and native secret-shared outputs (such as Prio3/VDAF) without in-circuit seed expansion; and
(3)~\emph{First-exposure subset defense}: reconciling and freezing complete eligible cohorts before any honest share becomes decryptable.
These dimensions delineate SHTPC's ecological niche:
SCIF~\cite{Su2025SCIF} enforces MPC input inclusion on memoryless inputs without client history policies or \(O(1)\) native bridges;
PEAR~\cite{Acharya2025PEAR} evaluates static NP predicates without atomic history heads, constant-size native binding, or first-exposure locking;
and Ball et al.~\cite{Ball2024Stateful} tracks global aggregate state rather than private per-client histories, leaving secret-share binding and subset exposure unaddressed.
SHTPC uniquely addresses the intersection of all three dimensions.

\par\noindent\emph{Input validity, inclusion and verifiable aggregation.}
VDAFs and DAP~\cite{Addanki2022PrioPlus,Barnes2023VDAF08,Geoghegan2026DAP,Davis2023VDAFPrivacy} decouple input validation from aggregation, extending to heavy hitters and streams~\cite{Boneh2021Poplar,Mouris2025Mastic,Rathee2024Whisper}. ELSA, RoFL and fully linear PCPs enforce input validity on shared data~\cite{Rathee2023ELSA,Lycklama2023RoFL,Boneh2019FullyLinear}, while VerSA and VeriFL verify aggregate correctness~\cite{Hahn2023VerSA,Luo2024VerSAComments,Guo2021VeriFL,Guo2022VeriFLFix}. Beyond input validity, SCIF~\cite{Su2025SCIF} provides honest-input inclusion in MPC, and PEAR~\cite{Acharya2025PEAR} evaluates zero-knowledge proofs for NP relations. SHTPC complements these systems by bridging zero-knowledge state proofs directly to native secret shares via masked one-hot index moments at \(O(1)\) circuit cost, assuming correctly executing services for integrity.

\par\noindent\emph{Stateful workflows, credentials and membership.}
While stateful aggregation~\cite{Ball2024Stateful} updates global aggregate state, SHTPC verifies private sequential transitions for individual clients. Proof-carrying data~\cite{Chiesa2010PCD,Bitansky2012Recursive} propagates computation history, while Privacy Pass, DAP report authentication, zk-creds and ZEBRA authorize credentials~\cite{Davidson2024PrivacyPass,Priebe2023ReportAuth,Rosenberg2023ZKCreds,Rathee2022Zebra}. In location services, VPriv, PrETP, PrivStats and ZKLP enforce charges, upload quotas or regional membership without revealing plaintext trajectories~\cite{Popa2009VPriv,Balasch2010PrETP,Popa2011PrivStats,Ernstberger2025ZKLP}. However, static credentials do not prevent replay against stale state; SHTPC atomically authorizes successors against the actual per-identity history head. Furthermore, unlike federated aggregation schemes that tolerate dropouts via dynamic subsets~\cite{Bonawitz2017SecureAggregation,Ma2023Flamingo,Bell2020SecAggPlus,So2022LightSecAgg}, SHTPC enforces strict release: because partial subsets leak history privacy across distinguishing trajectories, it freezes complete eligible cohorts before first share exposure, using durable server redelivery for transport faults.

\par\noindent\emph{Output privacy.}
Differential privacy~\cite{Dwork2014algorithmic,Bagdasaryan2022SparseHeatmaps} protects released heatmap statistics, complementing SHTPC's protocol-view protection.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Hidden-state validation is incorporated into private aggregation. SHTPC binds each admitted state transition to the contribution retained by aggregation roles using native one-hot validity and a compact masked-moment relation, extended to complete-cohort first exposure via atomic admission and durable reservation. End-to-end Janus controls exercise these interfaces, while component benchmarks and lifecycle measurements quantify their costs.

The design targets coordinated sampling applications requiring complete cohort statistics with delayed batch release, preserving strict membership without silent dropout or intersection release, while durable server redelivery handles client disconnections. All guarantees apply to admitted claims under the stated dual trust model without establishing physical GPS truth or Sybil resistance. Differencing attacks on exact cohort totals require orthogonal output-level differential privacy. Future directions include streaming deployment, continuous windows, and stateful profiles beyond heatmaps.

\label{lem:export-refinement}
The optimized export transaction refines Definition~\ref{def:trace-closure}. \par\noindent\emph{Native validity and key-tag assumption.}
Let \(Q_i\) count native stream queries by domain, in order measurement,
proof share, joint randomness, prover randomness, query randomness, joint
seed and joint part. Put \(Q=\sum_{i=1}^{7}Q_i\), and let \(P_{\rm att}\)
count distinct attack preparation inputs. The actual public key tag is a
96-bit prefix of the verification-key hash. For \(G_5\) external candidate-key
queries, let \(K\) be the uniform 128-bit key and \(Z=f(K)\) its tag.
On the no-hit branch, each tag value determines at most \(G_5\) candidate
keys \(E_z\). Averaging over tags gives
\[
 \Pr[K\in E_Z]=2^{-128}\sum_z|f^{-1}(z)\cap E_z|
 \le 2^{96}G_5/2^{128}.
\]
Thus \(\kappa_{\rm tag}\le\min\{1,G_5/2^{32}\}\), without assuming
uniform tag fibers. At \(G_5=2^{31}\), the term \(2\kappa_{\rm tag}\)
already saturates the information-only bound. The factor two charges the
two secret-key replacement hybrids. Primitive advantages remain symbolic.
An additional SHA256 one-wayness assumption on uniform 128-bit inputs gives
the alternative bound \(\kappa_{\rm tag}\le a_{\rm OW128}\), obtained by
scanning all queried keys with one additional hash per candidate. Collision
resistance alone does not supply it.
The native validity term is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 v_N=\min\bigl\{1,{}&10256(Q+P_{\rm att})/q+2\kappa_{\rm tag}\\
                   &+(2P_{\rm att}+Q)^2/2^{128}\bigr\}.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:native-validity-bound}
\end{equation}
Here \(10256=10002+254\). The 10,001-bin invalid-input identity contributes
at most \((10001+1)/q\) and a false gadget polynomial of degree at most 254
contributes \(254/q\). The root-abort branch remains in the experiment.
This invalid-input acceptance term is separate from the nonce-based privacy
bound below, since a corrupted role already knows the verification key.

\begin{proposition}[Effect of the exposure family]
\label{prop:exposure-family}
A fixed full cohort permits histogram-preserving reassignment. If every
size-\(k\) subset can instead be exposed, \(1\le k<n\), equal sums on
all such subsets force identical per-record native indices.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set \(d_i=\mathbf e_{y_i^{(0)}}-\mathbf e_{y_i^{(1)}}\).

choose a common \(k-1\) element set disjoint from \(i,j\). Exchanging \(i,j\) in a size-\(k\) subset gives \(d_i=d_j\).
A size-\(k\) sum then gives \(kd_i=0\), hence \(d_i=0\) over integers.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Concrete two-party native privacy]
\label{thm:concrete-two-party-native}

A single-world native stream simulation has loss at most
\begin{equation}
 b_N=\min\{1,(N^2+NQ_*)/2^{128}\},
 \label{eq:native-stream-bound}
\end{equation}
where \(Q_*=Q_1+Q_2+Q_5+2(Q_3+Q_4+Q_6)+4Q_7\).
Under Definition~\ref{game:privacy}'s exposure-time interface, the simulator
queries one complete cohort when the first honest share becomes
decryptable and none earlier, under Eq.~\eqref{eq:exposure-equivalence}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof intuition]
Fix either corrupt role's original measurement/proof shares, key and nonce.
Program fresh domain-separated streams, charging collisions and prior queries
to Eq.~\eqref{eq:native-stream-bound}. Sampling \(\Sigma\) from the verifier distribution

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joint privacy of admission and native release]
\label{thm:native-single-batch-privacy}
Use Definition~\ref{game:privacy}'s privacy experiment, source and oracle models, and primitive bounds.
Let \(X\) denote authority-only, active-role-and-Collector, or that aggregation view with authority business records.

Let \(N_{\rm id}\) count honest identifier draws and \(G_{\rm id}\) adversarial guesses. With \(C_X\) hidden HPKE encapsulations and labeled IND-CCA advantages \(H_X\), set
\begin{align}
 B_X&=\min\{1,g+\epsilon_H+a_X+c_B+N_{\rm id}G_{\rm id}/2^{128}\},
 \label{eq:route-bound}\\
 e_X&=\min\{1,2(C_X+10)B_X+H_X+b_N+t_N\},\\
 \operatorname{Adv}_X&\le\min\{1,2e_X\},
 \label{eq:native-joint-bound}
\end{align}
where \(t_N\le\min\{1,5N\binom{10129}{129}\rho^{129}\}\) and
\(\rho=(2^{128}-q)/2^{128}\). Authority-only needs no aggregate equality;
the other two views require Eq.~\eqref{eq:exposure-equivalence}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Hybrid outline]
Rows 1--4 of Table~\ref{tab:hybrid-ledger} sum to the bound in

\end{proof}
