# G4b: Complete Security Experiments, Ideal Interfaces, and Budget Semantics

Version: 2026-10-18, pinning the hardened G4a instance. This document specifies the complete security experiments used by all subsequent theorems; the supported inputs, trusted services, and execution model stated within these experiments are explicit premises. All resource budgets and allowed leakage are fixed prior to the challenge and may not be defined retroactively from observed successes, errors, or execution timings.

## 1. Parameters, Parties, and Setup Experiment

Let `D = 10000`, native vector space `V = F_q^(D+1)`, and `q = 340282366920938462946865773367900766209`; let `e_j` denote its standard basis vectors. An admitted claim `i` has public contribution bit `b_i ∈ {0, 1}` and hidden cell coordinates `(cx_i, cy_i) ∈ [0, 99]^2`, defining index `x_i = b_i(100·cy_i + cx_i) + (1 − b_i)D`. The native one-hot valid domain is `E = {e_0, ..., e_D}`; the final displayed histogram drops the sentinel bin `D`.

The parties are the owner set `U`, honest admission authority `A`, two aggregator roles `S_0`/`S_1`, Collector `C`, and Decoder. The task/profile/config, owner/epoch enrollment, role and Collector HPKE key handles, native verification key, the two current-relation CRSs, and all generators are fixed before any honest measurement. A historically published third related genesis key is also included in the adversary's auxiliary CRS in Document 02, but it does not belong to any currently accepted relation. This instance does not permit post-challenge key rotation or migration of legacy histories. The statically corrupted party set is fixed prior to the challenge. Recipient public keys are resolved by actual config ID to the same underlying key handle; an alias may not be treated as a distinct key. The prime order of the Groth16 source group / circuit scalar field modulus is denoted `p`; the BabyJub prime subgroup order is denoted `ℓ`; the native Field128 prime order is denoted `q`. These three moduli are never conflated.

Setup samples from the honest multi-relation setup distribution specified in Document 02, outputting public CRSs and key/config/manifest identifiers; `SimSetup` in the security reduction holds the simulated trapdoors. The real authority does not hold those trapdoors, nor is there any algorithm recovering trapdoors from the frozen public CRSs. Interpreting the saved development assets as a concrete instance is conditional on their originating from honest independent sampling without trapdoor leakage; hash checks and functional tests do not certify that condition. Public raw phase1, initial/final keys, contribution PoKs, and all permitted source points enter the typed generic group model of Document 02; no unknown correlated scalars or discrete-logarithm oracles are implicitly granted.

For an honest owner, the Pedersen commitment randomness for STATE/MASK/TICKET is sampled independently and uniformly in `F_ℓ`; the additive receipt mask is sampled independently and uniformly in `F_q`. These two randomness domains are never conflated. `RID` is 16 bytes generated by a CSPRNG, persistently reserved by the origin before the report is first made public, and globally unique within the task. Native sharding uses single-proof, two-party `Prio3Histogram(10001, 100)`, `Field128`, `XofTurboShake128`, and 16-byte seeds/nonces, governed by the concrete VDAF-08/DAP-09 domain encodings. The native XOF is analyzed in the programmable random-oracle model of Document 03; underlying signatures, hashes, HPKE, and independent curves each carry their own explicit assumptions, without deriving the security of one primitive for free from another.

## 2. Bounded Admissible Histories and Public Skeleton

Within finite execution budget `B`, the challenge environment supplies two complete admissible histories `H_0` and `H_1` for each honest owner. Each world has its own valid genesis, canonical bounded coordinates, STATE openings, predecessors, real `K = max(0, t − 6)` anchors, and a complete finite candidate chain; every step satisfies the ADWC integer relation, and `b = 0` reports enforce all state constraints as well. Any cross-window continuation proceeds from the actually committed head; pending records are candidates only. The adversary cannot alter the challenge inputs of an honest source retroactively based on previously received honest reports, though it may adaptively choose the next honest-source query from the pre-scheduled admissible history based on its existing view.

Both worlds must match the public skeleton `μ`: owners/epochs, fixed task/config/asset/key identities, windows, scheduled slot/accepted-step eligibility and public `b`, full predecessor/`K` reference topology, packet type/length rules, scheduled delivery boundaries/deadlines, and the statically corrupted set. `μ` excludes post-challenge commit/abort sets, actual verdicts, receipts, randomized commitment points, actual proof encodings/lengths, wall-clock execution durations, and individual measurements. Lengths of randomized points, proofs, and ciphertexts are computed from the actual bytes produced in the current simulation run; repeated references to the same history node sample once and reuse the handle, rather than invoking "public metadata" to mask correlations that still require simulation.

The adversary holds the keys of corrupted owners, may arbitrarily craft non-honest-source reports and chosen ciphertexts, and controls message ordering, delays, retries, peer inputs, drops, and aborts. Honest reports are delivered completely to the authority over an independent authenticated confidential channel according to the pre-scheduled input-ready timetable; local witness generation and proving durations lie outside the online interaction observation scope. Deployments that transmit immediately upon local measurement completion, thereby exposing local proving durations, do not inherit this boundary.

## 3. Complete Records and Ideal State F_lock

A typed record includes `(profile/config/asset/task/key-tag, owner/epoch, window/step, RID/body/statement/package identity, prev/K/next commitments, eligibility/b, native aggregation parameter)`; canonical field encodings follow the frozen source. For a selector, `P` denotes the locked complete record map, `R = eligible(P)`, `L` denotes the actually committed record map, and `A_L = eligible(L)`. Role `s`'s local committed projection is `L_s`, and its actually applied set is `M_s`. Each entry in `M_s` is additionally bound via immutable `body → native job/protected step → retained output/tag → consumed membership`. The lock record itself excludes not-yet-produced sessions or outputs, which are attached via receipts, `CommitCert`, and reservations.

`F_lock` maintains the following monotonic state without providing a secret verdict oracle:

1. `Register`: Verifies canonical public encodings, origin/body bindings, and relation witnesses, storing an independently complete finite candidate graph; before locking, items may be rejected under public rules or trigger whole-window failure. Each slot/RID admits at most one body; honest sources and adversarial sources are tracked separately.
2. `Lock`: Locks `P` and `R` only at the pre-scheduled boundary in `μ` and only when the complete `P` has been durably delivered; missing items cause waiting or whole-window abort, and `R` may never be pruned based on preparation feedback. Registration and locking do not advance actual history.
3. `Permit`: Checks dependencies and unique body against actual `head`/`K`/`version`/`quota`, issuing a permit with the same context as the lock; uncommitted successors never become real history.
4. `Prepare`: Pins the report, task, RID, body, nonce, aggregation parameter, and committed native state. The Helper persistently locks the first `job/step/peer input` and response in an explicit journal: an exact retry returns identical persistent bytes, while changed inputs or a new job trigger a public conflict before private processing. The Leader's native job/lease fence ensures at most one committed transition; its out-of-transaction uncommitted computations may receive differing Helper responses on retry without guaranteeing prior public conflict or durable Helper-style journaling of every protocol network message. All such feedback is simulated using the same pinned full-verifier/nonce/joint/query state of Document 03; uncommitted work never advances accepted history, retained state, or `apply`, while all physical calls and timings count against the budget. Identical RO descriptors reuse the complete raw candidate stream. The state machine advances accepted native state only upon final atomic commit; persistence boundaries for business receipts and retained responses are established in Document 05. Unknown measurement-dependent verdicts are never free inputs to `F_lock`.
5. `Receipt/Commit`: Generates masked receipts over the same retained output; after checking both receipt bindings and the moment equation, an atomic CAS on `head/K/version/quota` adds the unique valid successor to `L`. An adversarial role may sign an arbitrary receipt of its own, so Experiment `P` must simulate its verdict rather than assuming honesty. Failures preserve existing commits and consumed quota.
6. `Apply`: An honest role applies only the same retained output linked by `CommitCert`, inserting eligible items into `M_s` once; warmup advances state only, while eligible `b = 0` records remain members of `R` and `M`.
7. `Reserve/Send`: Requires type-identical completeness `L_s = P`, `eligible(L_s) = R`, `M_s = R`, and all matching retained links; persistently reserves a unique selector/snapshot and actual plaintext before sending an honest share. Cache paths pass through the exact same gate. Re-encryption may refresh ciphertexts, but cannot alter the cohort set or plaintext.
8. `Abort/Recovery`: Terminating prior to exposure records `PRE_EXPOSURE_ABORT`; once exposure has occurred or send was possible, zero exposure may never be claimed; `EXPOSED_COLLECTION_FAILED` preserves the exposure ledger and identical snapshot. Recovery may only resume from the same durable state without rolling back history/quota or clearing journal tombstones.

The task dispatch in this instance permits only one visible selector, so semantic output queries per task satisfy `E ≤ 1`. Multi-window histories may exist, but the current codebase does not establish repeated releases of distinct cohorts under the same task. Any generalized ideal multi-cohort setting must enforce equal sums per permitted cohort separately and maintain a dedicated query ledger; it does not follow from equality of a single final sum.

## 4. C: Semantic Closure Experiment

`Exp_C(Budget, B)`: After Setup, `B` controls all malicious owners and message delivery; the authority, both aggregators, Finalizer, Collector, and Decoder execute the protocol honestly. The native verification key `K` (16 B) is sampled uniformly prior to the challenge by honest setup and is never handed directly to malicious clients; the actually published `prefix96(SHA256(K))` leaks partial information accounted for under key-guessing in Documents 03 and 07, rather than treating `K` as having full 128 independent hidden bits. The only remaining `C`-visible use of `K` is the `U5` query-randomness interface; roles' private DBs and admin configurations are hidden from `C`, and corrupted-role capabilities from Experiment `P` may not be attributed to the `C` adversary. Process crashes and restarts are permitted, conditioned on trusted storage, transactional atomicity, and non-rollback recovery of committed state. `B` wins if at some prefix an honest decryptable share does not belong to the unique durable snapshot of the complete pinned `R`, or if a successful Decoder output disagrees with `Σ b_i e_(cell_i)` over the certified claims of complete `R`, or if `L` fails to form valid same-owner ADWC sequential histories.

Let `ε_C = Pr[Exp_C = 1]`. Document 05 establishes by deterministic induction that `B` cannot win once Document 02 extraction failures, commitment double-openings, honest-party authentication/canonical-hash collisions, and Document 03 native validity errors are excluded. Service bugs, disk corruption, administrative DB edits, or compiler violations of execution semantics are explicit execution-model failures, not absorbed into an undefined negligible `ε`.

Experiment `C` does not claim output correctness when a role is malicious: a corrupted aggregator can forge its masked moment or final aggregate share, so integrity and privacy below use distinct corruption sets.

## 5. A: Zero-Output Authority Experiment

`Exp_A^b`: Given Setup, statically corrupted owners, and admissible world `H_b`, the honest authority processes interactions permitted by `μ`. The observer receives all authority business inputs/outputs, public signals/proofs/bodies/ciphertexts/both receipts/diagnostics, authority business DB/WAL/backups, public state, and online timing. It knows the authority state and signing secrets held during normal authority execution, but obtains neither aggregator's decryption key, private inputs/outputs/DBs, nor any aggregate upon collection. `A` itself remains passive and executes signing honestly: its action interface neither permits signing extra control certificates, switching configs, or bypassing the state machine with its known authority key, nor allows colluding with malicious owners to inject such fresh authority signatures. The observer may run offline computations on its held key to distinguish views; normal authority signatures may be simulated via Setup or an honest signing interface without leaking that private key to another observer role. Its known authority key is excluded from the unknown-key set in the unforgeability reduction of Document 07.

The ideal simulator `Sim_A` takes `(Setup service interfaces, μ, adversarial messages sent so far, its own random coins)` and has ideal output interface `O_A = ∅`. It must generate the entire view online, without querying a histogram or using real wall-clock timestamps / abort bits after execution to backfill earlier prefixes. Worlds `H_0` and `H_1` are not required to yield identical aggregates, so this experiment cannot be obtained simply by projecting an aggregate-using `P+A` simulator.

Define `Δ_A(b)` as the statistical or computational distinguishing distance between `Real_A(H_b)` and `Ideal_A(μ)`, with advantage defined as the absolute difference in output-bit probabilities. Total two-world advantage is at most `Δ_A(0) + Δ_A(1)`. Document 04 specifies the zero-output simulator and per-hybrid gaps; perfect Pedersen hiding and the joint `(T, m_0, m_1)` distribution incur zero loss, while primitive substitutions and RO bad events are itemized in Document 07.

## 6. P and P+A: One Active Role and Collector

`Exp_P^b`: `B` statically controls one aggregator `c` and Collector `C`, and may control corrupted owners; the authority and role `h` are honest. `B` obtains `c`'s raw decryptable native shares/sidecars, all sent and received peer messages, public business messages, and the Collector's actual obtainable output. `B` may forge its own FLP share, joint part, receipt, or aggregate, and abort arbitrarily. As soon as any honest share becomes decryptable, the corresponding exposure enters the permitted exposure ledger regardless of whether collection ultimately succeeds.

`Exp_(P+A)^b` additionally grants the full business view of the honest authority, **without** granting the authority signing private key or allowing malicious signing. This does not merge with Experiment `A`: Experiment `A` knows its own internal secrets, whereas the augmented experiment shares business-level visibility only.

For each complete `R` actually authorized for first exposure, the ideal interface returns `O_C(R) = Σ_(i ∈ R) e_(x_i)`, including the sentinel, task/selector, and exposure ledger. If an interface only returns the sentinel-stripped vector, the `D` bin count is recovered from public `b` and `R`. Both admissible worlds must yield identical `O_C(R)` for every permitted exposed `R`; `c`'s forged actual Collector output is not required to match, nor are corrupted sources' native outputs assumed to obey their claims.

Partition sources into `H` (honestly generated by the challenge environment and recorded in the simulated query table) and `Bsrc = R \ H` (adversarial sources). Document 02 extraction or a priori knowledge provides the claims `x_j` for `Bsrc`; legitimate HPKE decryption and real native execution produce the honest retained outputs `w_(h,j)`. Only for `i ∈ H` does an honest Finish satisfy `v_(h,i) = e_(x_i) − v_(c,i)^orig`. Hence authorized ideal queries suffice to compute the true honest aggregate:

`V_h(R) = O_C(R) − Σ_(j ∈ Bsrc) e_(x_j) − Σ_(i ∈ R ∩ H) v_(c,i)^orig + Σ_(j ∈ Bsrc) w_(h,j)`.

This expression introduces no subset oracle on `H` and does not smuggle two-honest-role `C` integrity into `P`; the adversarial role's forged final share still feeds its own output actions. `Sim_P` / `Sim_(P+A)` in Document 04 may not query `O_C` prior to authorized first exposure of `R`, and afterwards may only read the cached identical value/plaintext. `Δ_P(b)` and `Δ_(P+A)(b)` are defined as in Section 5; Document 07 bounds per-world and two-world errors.

## 7. Budget B and Advantage Accounting

Budget `B` is publicly specified prior to Setup and challenge; all algorithms run at most `T` steps and issue at most the message counts and byte lengths allowed in `B`, halting according to public rules upon exhaustion. The budget is a resource bound of the finite security experiment, **not a claim that source code enforces identical hard caps on every interface**. In particular, genesis verifies proofs prior to exact-retry checks and therefore cannot be bounded by the 8-attempt per-origin transition cap.

At minimum, accounting separates: honest-source genesis proofs `N_g`; transition packet constructions `N_shard` (including warmup, zero, unprepared, excluded-from-final-`R`, failed, and prepared-then-aborted reports); physical Groth16 verifications `Q_g` / `Q_t` and per-hybrid simulation queries `S_g` / `S_t` / `S_old`; typed generic initial/derived handles and equality tests; per-key real signing/verification queries and total message bytes; total distinct-message queries `Q_H` to the single SHA-256 primitive (covering domain separation, packages, contexts, bodies, roots, and journals); per-recipient-key labeled HPKE encryption/decryption counts and byte budgets; distinct inputs, raw candidates, and known-seed re-expansions across the seven native XOF usages; honest `RID` and hidden seed/blind generations plus adversarial guesses; and each unique protected prepare attempt and first cohort exposure. All related-key simulation queries count toward `S = S_g + S_t + S_old`, even if legacy keys are rejected by current endpoints; unexposed legacy-key simulation APIs are never assumed by default.

Exact replays do not increment logical prepares or permitted outputs, but physical hash/signature/HTTP/RO recomputations still count toward physical work; cache reads of identical RO inputs do not draw fresh independent random samples, separating first-time queries from repeated reads. Document 03 relates raw candidates to accepted fields and supplies the strict-PPT truncation tail bound; Document 02 bounds generic denominators, degrees, and handles. Document 07 names every term with explicit units; all final expressions must be explicit finite formulas, never ending in `O()` or an undefined "total composition `ε`".

## 8. Standard Execution Layer and Timing Observations

The online executor emits an internal trace under the frozen target's instruction/address/IO semantics; integer add/subtract, fixed-width multiply, conditional select, fixed-length copies, and specified cryptographic primitives obey standard constant-time semantics, with the compiler preserving audited secret dataflow. Correspondence of custom receipt-core, native-field, and verdict paths follows from auditing actual source and compiled binaries rather than assuming primitive magic.

Rejection-sampling raw streams are cached by `(usage, key/seed, binders, offset)`; geometric rejection counts are co-generated with accepted values rather than resampled independently on the same seed or leaked as raw simulator timeouts. Secret `BYTEA` values are accessed by public keys at fixed lengths with compression disabled, export binaries are fixed-length, and status checks use SQL `EXISTS` only; `A` obtains neither honest roles' private DBs nor address traces.

Wall-clock timings, timeouts, and business aborts are causal post-processing of the simulatable internal work, adversarial scheduler, and world-independent executor coins; the simulator executes the exact same post-processing. This model grants no secret-dependent wall-clock oracle; co-resident cache contention, power/EM, faults, data-dependent hardware timing, arbitrary targets/compilers, and local prover generation times fall outside its coverage. Different hardware targets require re-establishing the execution mapping rather than substituting functional witnesses from this run.

## 9. Acceptance Boundaries
